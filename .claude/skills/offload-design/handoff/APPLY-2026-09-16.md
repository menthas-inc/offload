## 1. A value chip, and why its fill is not the point

**Taken as built, with one correction.** A value is `Chip`'s **geometry** with its own paint:

| | Light | Dark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fill | `--surface-card` (#FFFFFF) | `--surface-card` (#212121) |
| Border | `1px --border-strong` (#8C8C8C) | `1px --border-strong` (#777777) |
| Ink | `--text-primary` | `--text-primary` |
| Hover fill | `--surface-fill` | `--surface-fill` |
| Height | 30, inside a 44 target | 30, inside a 44 target |
| Type | `--size-label`, `--weight-medium` | same |

**The fill is not what makes this chip visible, and in dark it is not visible at all.** Measured
against the reply bubble, which is `--ink-50`:

- light: fill **1.06:1**, border **3.17:1**
- dark: fill **exactly 1.00:1** — the bubble and the card are both #212121 — border **3.59:1**

So **the border draws this chip in both themes** and the fill only matters where the bubble does not
reach: on the phones the bubble sits on `--surface-plate`, which is paper, so a filled chip and a
transparent one differ there. Do not "fix" the dark fill by reaching for a lighter surface; the
border is load-bearing by design and measures above 3:1 in both.

**This paint is not a `Chip` tone and must not become one.** `Chip` has `neutral` (a fill, 1.05:1
here, invisible on this ground) and `outline` (transparent over `--border-strong`, which is also the
turn footer's source anchor, so a turn that asked and read a page would draw the same object twice
with two meanings). What this needs from `Chip` is the 30-in-44 geometry, which `QuickReplies` now
copies explicitly. A governed component does not grow a variant for one use.

**No option in a question is ever a primary.** A choice among things it could be is a choice offload
does not know the answer to, and dark ink would say it did. 012's proposal keeps its primary,
because there offload does have a recommendation, so `kind: "primary"` stays in the contract.

## 2. The way out: last, its own line, and an `x`

```
kind: "plain"  →  transparent, no border, --text-secondary, --weight-regular,
                  padding 0 var(--space-3), and an Icon name="x" size={13} before the label
hover          →  --state-hover
```

**Two changes from what the clients have.** It carries an `x`, because plain and borderless beside
bordered values it reads as a value whose border failed to draw. And **it breaks to a line of its
own**: the values wrap among themselves, then a `flex-basis: 100%` spacer, then the plain options.

**Why the break is not cosmetic.** With five long labels the way out fell on a line of its own by
accident; with five short ones it landed beside a value. The arrangement was a property of the
labels rather than a decision, and a forced break is what makes it one. Verified at 390pt: five
short values wrap 4 + 1, and the way out is on line three.

## 3. Five, and the group's name

- **Five options at most**, wrapping to further rows. Anything past the fifth is dropped.
- **`label` is the sentence that asked, verbatim**, on a `role="group"`, so a screen reader hears
  the question before the options.
- **The phones repeat `label` as each chip's hint**, since iOS does not read a container's name.
  `QuickReplies` does this itself now: each option's `aria-label` is `"<label>, <hint>"`.
- **The 44 targets meet without gaps.** Each chip carries its own 44 cancelled by a `-7px 0`
  margin, so two wrapped lines sit 30 apart in ink and 44 apart in target. The gap between wrapped
  lines is `--space-4`; do not reduce it.

## 4. The typed invitation, and the only wording change

**All three strings in the entry overran the field.** Measured at 390pt in a 266px field: **309,
269 and 327px**. What each one lost was its example, which is the useful half of the sentence. So
the preamble goes:

| Question | Placeholder |
| --- | --- |
| a span | `Which dates? e.g. Sept 12th at 5pm` |
| a deadline | `By when? e.g. Sept 30th` |
| a day and time | `Which day and time? e.g. Friday at 5pm` |

The first is the product owner's own wording, verbatim. **One thing to check with them before
shipping it:** its example carries a time, which reads as an answer to the day-and-time question
rather than to a span. `Which dates? e.g. Sept 12th to 14th` is the same length if they want the
span example instead.

**The field's accessible name stays "Dump anything".** The placeholder is a hint; the question in
the bubble above is the question.

## 5. The question on a silent turn

A dump that filed, said nothing and needed one thing cleared up **gets a reply bubble for the
question**, with nothing else in it, **after** the turn footer that reports the filing.

Order is the turn's order: what happened, then what is still open. The filing is done and the
question is not.

**Not chips loose on the plate**, which was the alternative: the log's grammar is that a bubble is
somebody talking, and that would have made it the only sentence in the log outside one.

## 6. The reorder handle

`grip-vertical`, **vendored here as the 81st glyph** — `assets/icons/grip-vertical.svg`.

**Web (`WRow`):** first of the row tools, left of `pencil`, shown with them on hover and on focus.
**Phones (`ListRow`):** first in the trailing controls, beside the pencil. **A row with no pencil**
— a note, a memory — draws the handle alone at the trailing edge.

| State | Web | Phones |
| --- | --- | --- |
| resting | `--text-secondary` on `--surface-fill`, `grab` cursor | `--text-secondary` |
| being moved | row takes `--surface-fill` | row lifts: `--surface-raised` + `--shadow-2` |
| drop target | 2px `--ink-950`, row's **full width**, on the landing edge | same |

**Three things that are easy to get wrong.**

1. **The handle never inverts to ink on hover.** Every other tool in the row does, because it does
   something when pressed. This one does nothing when pressed, so it keeps `--surface-fill` and
   takes `grab`. It is the one control in the row whose hover says "hold me" rather than "press me".
2. **The drop line is full width, not inset to the text.** The system's own insets rule is that a
   row spans the full width and pays the gutter back inside, so an inset line would be the only mark
   in the list that does not follow it, and it reads as underlining the row above rather than as a
   gap between two.
3. **`--shadow-2` for the lift, never `--shadow-1`.** `--shadow-1` is the keyword `none` in the dark
   scope, so a lift built on it does not exist in dark. And do not write
   `box-shadow: var(--shadow-1), var(--shadow-2)` to cover both: `none` is not legal in a
   comma-separated shadow list and the whole declaration is dropped.

**The hit area is the 17px glyph plus 8px of slop**, not a full 44. That sits inside the row's own
target and is the compromise the row's other three controls already make.

**The drop is a priority**, the midpoint between the two neighbours on the 0 to 100 scale the
reprioritise already used, with a re-spread of the visible list when they are adjacent. Never a tie.

## 7. One height in the turn footer

**Every mark is 30**, named once as `MARK_H` so the next mark added cannot be a fourth height. The
row held 24, 26 and 30 side by side.

- Done mark: 30, `--size-label`, `padding 0 var(--space-5)`, check at 12
- Thread pill: 30 already, because it is a `Chip`
- Source anchor: 30, `--size-label`, `padding 0 var(--space-5)`

**30 because that is `Chip`**, the governed component the thread pill already comes from. The other
two were bespoke marks that happened to sit beside it, so they match the governed one rather than
all three meeting in the middle.

**And the row gains `margin-top: var(--space-3)`.** The log's own gap separates turns and does not
reach inside one, so the marks sat flush against the reply they describe.

## 8. The eyes go

**`state: "seen"` draws nothing.** The prop stays in the contract so a client can pass a message's
own state through without branching, but there is no mark, and a seen turn with no refs and no
sources **renders no row at all** — not an empty 26px gap.

The writing indicator says the same thing at the same moment a few lines below. That was two marks
for one state, and the one that stayed also says work is happening.

**This is the product's last emoji.** Three rules moved with it: the readme's emoji rule, now
"none"; principle 4 in the closing list, which said the marker stays; and the kits' `StatusChip` in
both phone kits, which drew it directly.

## 9. Two ways of declining ~~and they must not look alike~~ — **WITHDRAWN 18 September**

**Do not build this section.** It specified a second line in `--text-tertiary` under each short
reply: "Searching the web is off in Settings." under `Can't search that.`, and "That one is not
about your days." under `Not sure.`

**Code was right to withdraw it.** This system read the two lines as two states and they are not.
`Can't search that.` is the refused-subject line, said when the whole dump is about politics,
weapons, self-harm or sex, and it is said **whether or not the web switch is on**, so the second
line would have been untrue every time it appeared. `Not sure.` covers four states: a question
that is not about their days, a lookup that did not work, the web switch being off, and a country
offload does not know; the second line is right in one of the four.

**The honest version would have offload diagnose itself, which it does not do.** The sentence says
what happened and the screen offers the way forward.

**Both lines ship alone.** The kits' conversation fixtures draw them that way, and `readme.md`'s
Answering section now states the four states rather than a second line. The rest of this note
stands as written.

## 10. The done count is inside its control

One button, count inside it, **26 tall** — not 30. This is a fold inside a widget rather than a mark
in the turn footer, and it is not beside a `Chip`. `--surface-fill`, `--text-secondary`,

The fold is a preference of the member now, not of the thread, so what it remembers is per person.
Nothing drawn changes for that.
