Voice notes

021, read off main. The spec logs eleven items against the kit, which is what this page is: eight are code's own decisions drawn so you can see them, and three are corrections where the kit is wrong rather than merely behind. Both themes, real size, on the components as they are.

The one rule everything follows: a voice note is words. Once offload has written it down the message is a text message to every reader — the classifier, the filing turn, the tail, 018's judge, search, 013's answering — and none of them has a rule of its own for voice. So there is no new screen here, only a composer state, a bubble and a transcript.

And the labela one-word fix
The kit names the discard control "Discard recording".

The vocabulary bans that noun: a person records a voice note, and the thing is a voice note. There is no "recording", "audio", "clip", "memo" or "attachment" anywhere a person reads.

So: "Discard voice note", and the microphone keeps the kit's own "Voice note".
  • It is a screen reader's whole description of that button, which is the one place the banned noun would have been read aloud.

2. The sent note

the bars, and what they mean

The bubble joins the conversation at the press, as SentVoice. What changes is what the bars are and what they say.

As the kit draws ita decorative ramp
0:14
--ink-950 under a 0.3 to 0.9 opacity ramp
  • The ramp means nothing. Every bar is --ink-950 and the opacity climbs left to right, so the note appears to fade in from silence whatever was actually said. It reads as a played fill that is always half played.
  • And it retypes a ramp step where a role exists. --ink-950 in a bubble is --text-primary's value by accident, not by contract.
  • Its fallback draws 26 bars from a sine when it has nothing, where the composer's own WAVE_BARS is 32. The crop on the left is that fallback, which is what the kit shows today for every sent note.
Corrected32 bars, two roles
0:14
  • Thirty two bars resampled over the whole note, computed on the device at stop, sent with the upload and stored, so every surface and every reload draws the same bars and a bar is a fixed slice of time.
  • At rest every bar is --text-tertiary, 4.76:1 on the bubble, which is the honest resting state: nothing has been played and the whole waveform is the note.
  • No sine fallback. A note always has its bars, because they arrive with it; a drawn sine is a picture of a different recording.

Playingnew state
0:08
0:08
  • A played bar is --text-attention, the bubble's own blue, and the fill runs left to right while the duration counts down. Not --text-primary: ink is louder in the absolute but it measures 1.21:1 against a tertiary remainder, so the boundary between played and not was the one thing you could not see.
  • The remainder recedes to --ink-300 only while playing, which is the whole of the trick: there is a fill to read it against then, so the boundary measures 3.53:1 light and 3.92:1 dark. A row at rest keeps its full waveform — dimming it there would cost the default state of every note 3 points of contrast to buy legibility in a state it is not in.
  • The glyph is the control, so it says what pressing it does: play at rest, pause while playing. A microphone there says record, which is the composer's job and not this row's. Both vendored in this pass, the 83rd and 84th.
  • One plays at a time across the screen, and the bubble is inert while the composer records, so playback never feeds the microphone.
  • The row's own target is 44px minimum, and its name is "Voice note, 0:14, play" with state "playing, 0:08 left".

DiscardedUndo
Discard sits a thumb's width from Send, and a nine-minute thought is not something to lose to a mis-tap.
  • Eight seconds, the product's own Undo, with the note kept on the device until the toast clears. A dialog would ask you to confirm every discard to protect the rare long one.
  • Only past a minute. A ten-second note is cheap to say again, and a toast after every discard is a toast nobody reads.

Android's pair, and the web's width
Androidon the Material pair

Oak brackets, 30cm, matte black
0:14
Buy oak brackets, thirty centimetres, matte black
0:14
  • Two tokens in the composer, and that is the whole of its divergence. --a-on-var for the time and --a-on for the bars, which is all M3Composer.jsx changes. The sent bubble is not one of them: Conversation.tsx's Yours has no Android branch at all, so the person's words are --blue-50 over --blue-100 in --ink-950 on both platforms. Only the reply bubble, the question bubble and the writing dots take --a-c. The pill itself is the shared one: --radius-pill, --surface-raised, --border-focus, identical to iOS.
  • Which is the rule from the 21st read the right way round. "An Android override that is not an --a-* token is almost certainly wrong" does not mean every --a-* token is right: M3Composer deliberately keeps the shared pill, so giving it --a-c-low and --radius-xl would invent a divergence the source does not have. The dot stays --text-danger and the transcript --text-secondary for the same reason.
  • What does diverge is the platform's own chrome, which is not the product's to share. M3Phone.jsx draws a tighter status bar, 10px 18px 2px on --surface-raised with no blur, medium 12 on --a-on and a narrower battery, against iOS's 12px 26px 4px semibold on blurred --surface-chrome. And the gesture bar is 108 by 4 at --a-on-var where iOS's home indicator is 134 by 5 at --ink-300.
  • Which is the line the rule draws. A component the product owns is shared unless its own source says otherwise; the frame around it belongs to the platform, and there Material is the authority.

What this asks for

three corrections, and one measurement
  • Three places the kit is wrong rather than behind, all in section 1 and 2: typed words hidden while being sent; the discard control using the banned noun; and SentVoice's bars on an opacity ramp over a retyped ramp step, with a 26-bar sine fallback where the composer says 32.
  • Everything else is code's decision drawn, and I would take all of it. The stopped row, the transcript in the bubble, the playing state, the four failure lines, the Discard toast and the quick action's arrival.
  • One thing the spec lists as needing a device that does not: the dark transcript contrast. Measured here: 7.65:1 in dark and 6.24:1 in light, --text-secondary on the sent bubble, which resolves to #161F42 in dark. Show all is 5.72:1 on the same ground. So that line can come off the device list.
  • And the two bar states measure as intended: played 18.37:1 light and 13.71:1 dark, at rest 4.76:1 and 5.36:1. The distinction is legible in both, which is what the played fill depends on.
  • Not drawn, because nobody has asked: what the composer looks like at the largest text size with three lines of words above the row, which is the tallest this pill can get. The spec lists it under "needs a device" and it is a layout question, not a device one.
