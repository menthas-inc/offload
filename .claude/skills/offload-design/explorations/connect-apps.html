Connect apps, onboarding A chosen

A is the direction, kept at the top; B and C stay below as the record of what it was chosen against. The screen sits at the end of first run, it is optional, and it does not say Skip, because Skip belongs to the name screen and means skip the tutorial; a connection you decline is Not now. The Google Calendar mark is a drop slot rather than something I drew: hand-tracing a brand mark gets the geometry and the colours slightly wrong, and slightly wrong is worse on a logo than anywhere else. Drop the official file onto A's slot and it stays there. In the kits the mark is a prop and the expected path is assets/brands/google-calendar.svg, recorded in assets/brands/README.md; until something is passed they draw a dashed stand-in, so a missing asset is visible rather than silent.

None of the copy on these screens is final. Every string is placeholder for the app to decide, and the two lines under the mark most of all: they read as a promise about what offload does to your data, which is a product commitment rather than a design one. One of them has already moved. Writes are allowed as of 8 September, so the old “never writes to them” guarantee is gone from every screen here and from web Settings. What replaces it is a placeholder, not an answer.

What each one commits you to

and the two questions underneath
  • The logo is a drop slot on A, deliberately. I can draw offload's own mark from Logo.jsx because the geometry is ours and written down. Google Calendar's is not: the corner radius, the four edge colours and the numeral's weight are all specified by somebody else, and an approximation that is 90% right is the one kind of wrong a person notices instantly on a logo they see every day. Drop the official SVG on A's slot and it persists. B and C carry a neutral stand-in, since they are the record rather than the deliverable.
  • Placement decides how large the mark can be, and that is the real trade. A is 88px, which is the only size at which somebody scanning the screen knows what is being asked before reading a word. B is 44px because a row is 72px tall. C is 28px, because in C the agenda is the argument and the mark only labels where it came from.
  • None of them says Skip. The 7 September entry moved Skip to the name screen, where it means skip the tutorial. Declining a connection is not skipping a step, so it is Not now — same position as Skip, top right, different word.
  • Connecting is a step, so the tutorial's last button says Next. Nothing has started at that point, and "Get started" on a card with another card after it is a promise the flow does not keep. That also settles the indicator question the 8 September entry left open: the dots span the tutorial plus this screen, four on both platforms.
  • The two lines under the mark are the only copy here that makes a promise, and promises are the app's to make. They are placeholder. The first says what offload reads; the second said it would never write, and that is now wrong: writes are allowed, so it says offload can add to your calendar instead. Both need the app's wording before this ships, and the second needs more than wording. If offload writes, somebody will want to know what appears, under whose name, and whether it can be undone, and none of those are answered by a sentence on an onboarding screen. It may need its own row in Settings rather than a line here.
  • Two facts, not a feature list, is the part worth keeping. Whatever the strings become, the shape holds: what it reads, what it does back. Both as sentences rather than toggles, per the Privacy refusal from 5 September, since a switch implies somebody could have set it the other way.
  • The indicator is FirstRun.jsx's, but the count of five is an assumption of mine. The shape is the source's: 6px tall, the active step an 18px pill, inactive 6px circles. The source maps TUTORIAL and therefore renders three, one per tutorial screen. Five here says the indicator now spans the whole of first run, tutorial plus name plus this, and nothing decides that: the 7 September entry adds the name screen without saying whether it joins the dots. If it does not, this screen has no indicator at all, which is also a reasonable answer for an optional last step.
  • One question I cannot answer for you. Whether this screen appears at all for an account that already existed. The name screen already has two arrivals, first run and its own standalone launch, and a connection prompt has the same shape: everybody in the alpha is an existing account, so the standalone version is the one that will actually be seen.
  • A second, smaller one. Whether declining is remembered. If Not now means never asking again, the Settings row is the only way back and should be findable; if it means asking later, the screen needs a rule for when, and “every launch until you say yes” is how a prompt becomes something people learn to dismiss without reading.
