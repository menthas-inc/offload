Sign in, first run, landing

Your three calls drawn out: Apple and Google as the only way in, flow B's three tutorial screens on first login, and an invite-only landing page with a waitlist. Every page is shown on web, iOS and Android at real size, scaled down together. Nothing is wired yet, this is the thing to confirm before it goes into the kits.

1 · Sign in

the gate, nobody sees the app before it

One screen, two buttons, no email field and no password anywhere. Order flips by platform: Apple leads on iOS, Google leads on Android, and web shows Apple first with Google directly under it. The invite check happens after the provider returns, so the waitlist line sits under the buttons rather than in front of them.

  • Two providers only. No email link, no password. Someone whose invite went to an address they cannot sign in with is a support case, not a screen.
  • Apple's rule means the Apple button has to be at least as prominent as Google's wherever Google appears on iOS, so iOS leads with Apple. Android leads with Google because that account is already on the device.
  • Returning users see this same screen. The provider sheet does the remembering, so there is no separate sign-in-again design.

Three screens after the first successful sign-in: what you put in, what offload does with it, when it speaks. Dots and Skip sit at the top, one button at the bottom, and Get started on the third screen lands on Today. Web runs the same three screens as a centred column; iOS advances with the push transition, Android with shared axis X.

Get started
  • Get started dismisses the flow and lands on Today, which is empty except for the composer. The tutorial never comes back, and it is not repeated per platform: sign in on the phone first and the web does not show it again.
  • The notification ask fires right after Get started on mobile, so the third screen is the reason it appears. Web has nothing to ask for.
  • Skip jumps straight to Today from any screen. It stays in the same place on all three screens so it never moves under the cursor or thumb.
