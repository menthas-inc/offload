Threads — ten icons

All at tab-bar size with the label, and larger above. The idea to carry is a boundary drawn round related things — and the boundary is offload’s, so it can move.

Threads circlein use

A boundary drawn round related things. The current pick, clean, but it carries no idea of its own.

Threads circle-dashed

Same boundary, drawn loosely. The dashes say the edge is provisional, offload can still redraw it.

Threads blend

Two overlapping circles. Says a thread is shared, and where things belong to more than one.

Threads circle-dot

A boundary with something at its centre, a thread has a subject.

Threads group

A selection frame round a cluster. Reads as "these belong together" most literally.

Threads waypoints

Nodes joined by a line. A thread as a path through connected things.

Threads git-branch

A literal thread that splits. Precise, but developer-flavoured.

Threads lasso

A loop thrown round a group by hand. The most human, the least geometric.

Threads orbit

Things circling a centre. Distinctive, but reads as space or AI.

Threads spline

An open curve with handles. Abstract and elegant; means little on its own.

My picks, in order. circle-dashed is the smallest change with the most meaning: the same shape you have, but the broken edge says the grouping is provisional and offload can redraw it — which is exactly the product’s promise. blend is next, because shared threads are core to offload and two overlapping circles say that in one glyph. group is the most literal and the most legible at 22px. I would rule out orbit (space/AI cliché), git-branch (developer) and spline (means nothing without context).
