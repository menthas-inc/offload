Memories — ten icons

The tab bar as it stands, then every option at tab size. The three glyphs have to work as a set, so each candidate is judged next to house and the lasso.

Memories brainin use

Literal, this is the app remembering for you. The current pick. Clear, but it is the icon every AI product reaches for.

Memories sparkles

offload’s own mark for “we did this”, already used for inferred tags. Consistent, but overloaded and very AI-generic.

Memories clock-fading

Time, dissolving. The one glyph here that actually means “memory” rather than “storage” or “a brain”.

Memories archive

Everything filed away and retrievable. Honest, but sounds like cold storage rather than something alive.

Memories gallery-vertical-end

A stack of everything you have put down, seen edge-on. Quiet, abstract, no cliché.

Memories album

A photo album with a bookmark. Warm and personal — memories as something you look back through.

Memories library-big

Shelves of things you kept. Suggests a searchable body of knowledge.

Memories rewind

Going back through what happened. Distinctive, but reads as media playback.

Memories message-circle

What we had for Thoughts. Fits a question-and-answer screen, fits “memories” poorly.

Memories search

Drop the metaphor: the tab is where you go to find things. Boring and completely unambiguous.

My picks. clock-fading is the only glyph here that means memory rather than storage — and next to a hand-drawn lasso it keeps the set feeling human rather than technical. gallery-vertical-end is the strongest abstract option: a stack of everything you put down, no cliché. album if you want warmth. I would move off brain — it is the default AI glyph and it makes the tab about the software rather than about your own stuff.
