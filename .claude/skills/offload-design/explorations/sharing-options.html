Sharing, and invites

Sharing a thing with someone, in both directions. Section 1 is where the affordance lives, then the two ways to send that both ship, the three ways to receive, and the @ picker that every text field in the product gets. Sharing never lets anyone new into offload: you can only share with an existing connection. Mobile is drawn in the iOS kit's chrome, Android is the same flow with the Material bars and a filled blue primary, which is a mechanical swap. Nothing is wired.

1 · Where it lives

one affordance per thing, nowhere else

Sharing belongs to a thing, not to a note or a task inside it, and not to the app as a whole. On the phone the thing's bar carries the people, on web the pane head does. Both show who is already in before offering to add anyone, because the most common question is not "how do I share" but "who can see this".

4 · @ picks a person

the composer, and every other field

Typing @ anywhere you can type opens a small list of your connections, filtered as you keep typing, the way it works in a chat app. It is the same list the share sheet shows, which is why sharing has no separate people-picker screen. In the composer it turns a sentence into an action; in a note or a task title it just leaves a person attached to what you wrote.

ask @an
  • One list, everywhere. Connections only, ordered by how much you share with them, filtered as you type. Three rows at a time on the phone, no scroll.
  • The mention resolves to the person, not to a string: it renders as the name in accent ink, and offload can act on it, "ask @ana about the dates" or "share this with @tom".
  • Escape and a space both dismiss it, and an unmatched @ stays as plain text, because email addresses exist.
  • Not designed here: what a mention does inside a note the other person can see, and whether mentioning someone notifies them.

Rules

  • One permission, per thing. They can add, they can never delete what is yours. No roles, no viewer and editor, no per-item toggles. The one sentence appears identically in the sheet, in offload's reply and on the invite screen.
  • Leaving is symmetrical. Anyone can remove themselves from a thing, the owner can remove anyone, and both cases toast with an undo like every other destructive action.
  • What offload says about other people's work stays in the third person and never editorialises: "Ana added two things", not "Ana has been busy".
  • You can only share with a connection. Sharing never lets anyone new into offload, and there is no public link. Someone without an account has to come through the waitlist like everyone else, which is why the sheet's last row makes a connection rather than sending an invitation.
  • Not designed here: the notification copy for each share event, and what a shared thread looks like in Memories, where offload's inferences would start mixing two people's material.
