Onboarding, reimagined

First run as it is on main today, then three shorter ways through it. All three share one new animated intro, a footer where Skip sits beside the main button, titles at 34 to 36px on the phones and 44 to 52px on the web, and asking screens on the plain page with no prism behind any text.

  • Seven screens after sign in, eight with the system dialog. Connecting a calendar ends first run on the Calendar settings page, not Today.
  • The tutorial's text is small for the room it has. The illustration takes the flexible space and the copy sits underneath at title-1 and 16px body. The illustrations are three static pictures, and nothing on them moves or shows threads being shared or the ways in.

  • Skip is a 15px text label top right; Next is a full-width filled button at the bottom. Not now, on Mail and Calendar, is the same top-right label. Moving on is under the thumb and declining is a reach.
  • Two offers, two screens (Forward what matters, Bring in your calendar), each with a large mark, two facts and one button.

The intro, shared by all three

replaces the three tutorial screens

One screen that plays itself. Three scenes of about five seconds, with story-style bars at the top. Tap the art or Next to move on, or Skip intro to leave. Each scene acts out its sentence: four loose dumps fall into a thread; a thread gains Ana and Tom and Tom adds something; a photo, a voice note, an email and a link all land in the same thread. The prism blooms behind the art only, and the copy sits on the page below it. With reduced motion the scenes cut without animating. Help's See the tutorial again plays this screen and nothing else.

Three ways through

after sign in

A. Intro, you, connect

3 screensmy pick
Intro→Before you start: name and consent→Bring things in: Mail, Calendar, notifications→Today

The name joins the consent: both are required, and they are the only things first run has to ask. Mail, Calendar and notifications become three cards on one optional screen, each with its own button, so you can connect one and skip the others. The notification dialog moves here. Its reason used to be the third tutorial screen, which the intro drops, so the card is now the reason. Connecting Calendar returns to this screen with a Connected pill rather than ending on the Calendar page.

B. Intro, then one setup screen

2 screens
Intro→Two things, then you are in: name, consent, optional connections→Today

The fastest. Everything after the intro is on one scrolling screen: two numbered required steps, then the connections as three compact rows marked Optional. There is nothing to skip, because the optional part is skipped by not tapping it. The cost is length: the screen scrolls on a small phone, and Get started sits under a consent and three rows.

C. Intro, you, first dump

3 screens
Intro→Before you start→Dump your first thing→Today, with ways in offered

Learn by doing. The last screen is a real composer with the day-one examples as starters. Sending one files it for real, and you watch it land in a thread before going to Today. Connections leave first run entirely and become two dismissible cards on Today, under what you just dumped, where they make sense next to real content. The notification dialog fires the first time something you dump has a date.

To decide

  • Direction: A, B or C. My read: A. It is short, keeps the legal screen uncluttered, and gives every optional thing its own button and an obvious Skip for now.
  • Footer: side by side or stacked.
  • The intro's copy is my draft from your three points; the consent text is verbatim and stays so.
