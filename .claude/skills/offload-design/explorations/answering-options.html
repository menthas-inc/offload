Answering a question

Mocks for 013, drawn from the server that is already built: one reply, up to three results, up to twenty rows each, and rows that are resolved on read rather than baked into the message. Everything here is the phone, because the phone is where the answer has the least room and every question the brief left open is a question about room. Web follows once the shapes are settled. The three constraints I took as given: results sit below the reply bubble and never inside it, a result names its thread once and in one place, and nothing in an answer can destroy anything.

What this is meant to answer

read this first
  • ROWS_SHOWN should be 5 on the phones, not 8. The log is about 446pt on a 844pt phone, and eight rows with the header and the control overrun it by roughly 165pt, so you land on rows with the sentence that answers the question already above the top edge. Section 3 draws 5, 8 and 12 at real size. Web has roughly twice the height and can keep 8.
  • The chip becomes the card's header row. The rows get a surface of their own and the thread names them from inside it, which keeps one link per result and gives the count somewhere honest to live. It also bounds the hover fill, so web needs no exception to the pane row's negative margin. Section 2.
  • A truncated read ends in the thread, and says the number it actually saw. Not a Show more, because there is nothing more to show. Section 4.
  • The done state loses its opacity here and everywhere. Tertiary ink, a 1px strike and the moss tick, which is what both shipping components already do and what the kits still get wrong. Section 5.
  • Three things a turn already carries collide with a result. The Done mark must not appear on an answering turn, a result goes below the bubble while 012's control stays inside it, and the filed chips are empty when nothing was filed. Section 6.
  • Follow-ups are 012's control generalised, and the best one is not asked at all. Quick replies, object pickers, staleness, and the three accelerators that are not a chip row. Section 7.

a treatment, not an error

Neither is an error state and neither gets a warning colour. A refusal is a short sentence in the same bubble as any other answer, and the difference between them is what the sentence describes.

  • Dimming a row on a warm page reads as a rendering fault, and it takes the label under the contrast floor on the way. The meta line here is the part that fails first.

The rules this all rests onfor the readme
  • Three options at most, and every one of them is typeable. The labels are the words somebody would say.
  • A proposal has one primary and one plain way out. A choice among equals has no primary. Dark ink means offload is recommending, so it may not appear on a question where offload does not know.
  • No option destroys anything. Nothing that deletes, archives or empties a thread is ever a chip. It is the same rule as AC-6a and for the same reason.
  • Picking writes your turn into the log exactly as typing would, and the turn it creates carries the write tools. The turn that asked carries none. A sentence in a shared thread can therefore never talk offload into a write: the tools arrive with a human tap.
  • One open question at a time, closed by the next turn whatever it says.
  • Ask only what Undo cannot cover. Not reversible, reaches somebody else, or a genuine coin flip. Everything else is done and reported.
