Settings

Same nine categories in each, so the difference is purely how you get to them. Every option is a standalone page behind the gear.

Categories: My account (name, email, password, sign out, delete). My people (who you share with, invites, blocked). Communication preferences (what offload interrupts you for, quiet hours, email, push). Connected apps (calendar, mail, photos, browser). Memory and data (what offload keeps, retention, export, wipe). Privacy (who sees what, sharing defaults). Appearance (text size, theme). Language and region (units, week start, date format). Help and about (support, version, legal).

Added beyond your three: connected apps, memory and data, privacy, appearance, language and region, help and about. Retention belongs here since the conversation list already promises 60 days, and connected apps matters because offload reads calendars and mail.

1. One list, grouped by rule

Every category is a row under the same section headers used elsewhere in the app, each with an icon and the current value on the right. Nothing new to learn, and the value column means you can answer most questions without opening anything.

2. Profile first, then cards

Opens on who you are, then the categories as filled cards with quiet labels between them. The account row disappears because the header is the account. Softer, and the grouping reads faster, but it invents a card style the app does not use elsewhere.

3. Search first, common things up top

A search field, then the four switches people actually come here for, then the categories as a plain list. Treats settings as a place you arrive with a task, not a place you browse. Costs vertical space and duplicates rows.

4. Tiles, then the rest

The three categories you named as tiles with a line of description, so the important ones are chosen rather than ranked, and the housekeeping sits below as a list. Most distinctive; the tiles cost height and the descriptions go stale.
