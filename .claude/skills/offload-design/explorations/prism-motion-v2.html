3 · Real distortion, per pixel

The field genuinely warped: every pixel samples the texture at an offset from an animated flow, so the shapes stretch and fold rather than translate. This is what you cannot fake with transforms. On device it is a react-native-skia runtime shader, which is cheap per frame but adds several MB to the bundle and keeps the GPU render loop awake for as long as it runs.

The distortion question, answered

what is actually cheap on a phone
ApproachPer-frame costWhat it costs you
Scale-differentiated layers
frame 1		nothingThree baked images, about 340KB as PNG and roughly half that as WebP. No shader, no sampling, no JS. Reads as warping because the layers beat against each other at different scales.
A baked lens
frame 2		nothingOne more image, 47KB, with the radial falloff already in its alpha so nothing is masked at run time. Gives local magnification but the magnification never changes: it is a fixed lens moving, not a lens that deforms.

frame 3		one GPU pass over the bandSeveral MB of bundle, and the render loop stays awake while it animates, which is the part that shows up on battery. The shader itself is trivial work: a fragment sampling a texture at an offset is what a GPU is for.
SVG filter, as the site does~96ms, measuredNot available on React Native without Skia anyway, and this is the exact thing that made the web atmosphere have to freeze behind the privacy page. Ruled out.
Sprite sheet of warp framesnothingPre-render the warp offline and step through frames. Genuinely free per frame, but a fluid warp needs 40+ frames to not read as a flip book, which is megabytes. Mentioned for completeness; not recommended.
  • The honest comparison is frame 1 against frame 3. Turn the warp slider to 0 and frame 3 becomes a still image, which is the fair baseline. What Skia buys is shapes that stretch and fold; what three layers give you is shapes that pass over each other. Both are unique over time. Only one of them costs a dependency.
  • Frame 2's lens is the weakest of the three. It magnifies, but by a fixed amount, so once you have watched it cross once there is nothing new in it. The three-layer pile keeps producing combinations because the periods are coprime enough not to resynchronise for hours.
  • If you do take Skia, take it for the gate and not the app. The gate is on screen for seconds at a time with the field across the whole top, so a live shader there is both visible and short-lived. Behind the plate it is a warp nobody can see, running all day.
  • The real question option B asks is whether TabBar may stop being opaque. Its contract is 64px, a hairline top border, background: --surface-chrome-strong, and backdropFilter: --blur-chrome, with the active label at --text-primary over inactive at --text-tertiary. So B's premise, that the plate gives up its own fill, was a divergence from that contract from the start, and the 86% bed in these frames is not a new compromise: it is that contract with one value changed.
  • And the component was written expecting this. TabBar already carries --blur-chrome, saturate(140%) blur(14px), which does nothing at all today because --surface-chrome-strong resolves to opaque --surface-raised. The blur is sitting there waiting for a translucent chrome token. That is the decision in front of you: whether --surface-chrome-strong gains an alpha, in which case --blur-chrome starts doing its job and every chrome surface in the product, tab bar, app bar, sticky section headers and the composer, changes together. It is a token decision, not a tab bar decision.
  • The label colours need no change either way, and my earlier note that they did was wrong: it measured against an --ink-400 inactive state that exists nowhere in the kit. At 86% the contract's own --text-tertiary holds 4.71:1 over the field at 100% density and 4.60:1 at the 140% cap, keeping the 3.86:1 separation from the active label that the component already ships with.
  • Density is capped at 140%, and the cap is the contrast floor rather than taste. At 140% the inactive label still clears everywhere at 4.91:1; at 200%, which the slider used to allow, it drops to 3.62:1 and fails part of the strip. Within 30 to 140 it is a purely aesthetic dial.
  • The composer needs no contrast argument at all, and my earlier one was about a bed that does not exist. Composer.jsx's pill is already opaque --surface-input with a 1px --border-focus hairline, so the placeholder sits on #F2F2F2 and never meets the band. What diverges is the composer row, which gives up --surface-chrome; the pill inside it keeps its fill exactly as specified. That makes the option simpler than two rounds of my own measurements suggested: no text in the composer is ever over the field.
  • Reduced motion stops all of it, and frame 3 shows the gate rather than claiming it. The CSS animations are cut by a media query; the warp cannot be, because a media query has no way to stop a requestAnimationFrame and no way to notice a backgrounded tab. So the loop checks matchMedia and document.hidden, paints one still frame at a fixed clock when either says stop, and restarts on visibilitychange. That gate is not mock scaffolding: it is the code you would have to write on device, and leaving it out is how a live shader ends up running in a backgrounded app.
  • One line in one file is the entire divergence, and it is not a token change. App.jsx composes the bottom plate as a single wrapper carrying background: --surface-raised, --radius-xl top corners and --shadow-sheet, and it passes bare to Composer and background: transparent to TabBar. So neither child has a fill to give up: on the phones they are already transparent. The only thing standing between the band and the plate is that wrapper's own --surface-raised, which is 86% in these frames. Everything else on screen, including the app bar's opaque chrome and the composer's --surface-input pill, is exactly as specified.
  • Which makes this much cheaper to try than I said two rounds ago. I had it as a --surface-chrome-strong question rippling through the tab bar, app bar, sticky section headers and composer together. It is not: those surfaces are untouched, and the change is one property on one wrapper on one screen. The contrast work still stands, because the labels sit on 86% white over the field either way, but the cost of adopting it does not.
  • The dump nudge is unchanged and works on all three, since it sits on its own layer above the drift and below the scroll offset.
