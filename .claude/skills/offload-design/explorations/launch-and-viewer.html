The launch screen, the viewer, and six owed screens

Everything the 21 September pull left owed, drawn at real size in both themes. The launch screen is the one with no entry behind it, so it is first and it is the only section that turned up a conflict: neither platform's splash can show a spinner, so the thing you picked is two screens rather than one. The rest are drawings of decisions code has already made, and the only one that needs a new token is the photo viewer's plane, which is hand-mixed in light and an existing token at full opacity in dark.

Photos are drop slots. Nothing on this page has a real picture in it: drag your own onto any tile or the viewer and it stays. The tiles are drawn at the real geometry either way.

1. The launch screen

the mark, on the app's own ground

The mark alone, centred, on --surface-page, in both themes. No wordmark: iOS's storyboard allows no text at all, and the wordmark is live type rather than an outlined file, so it could not be in a splash even if the platform allowed one.

  • The ground is --surface-page: #FAF9F7 in light, #1C1B19 in dark. The mark is --ink-950 and --paper, which is the one-colour rule the mark has always had.
  • The mark is 216pt on iOS and 192dp on Android.   • the thing to know A spinner cannot go on the native splash. iOS gives you a background colour and one static image; Android 12 and later gives you a colour, an icon and its own reveal, and neither takes a view of yours. So "a spinner after about a second" is a screen the app draws the instant the splash hands over, identical to it, with the spinner appearing on a timer from launch.
  • The seam is the whole risk. Two screens meant to look like one will not, unless the mark is at the same size in the same place in both, measured from the screen's centre rather than from a container. The spinner must not be in the centring flow: put it in the column and its box re-centres everything above it, which moves the mark by half the spinner's height plus its gap. In these two frames that would be 37px, and it is a blink on every cold start. Here the mark centres on the screen and the spinner is placed under it.
  • The spinner waits 1000ms and is unlabelled. The readme allows a lowercase label and there is nothing honest to put in it: offload is not looking through your stuff yet, it is finding out who you are. Under a second, nobody sees it and the start reads as instant; over a second, the screen says the app is alive rather than stuck.
  • It sits 64px under the mark, which is clear space enough that the two never read as one object, and it is the system's own Spinner rather than the platform's.
  • two whites, two seconds apart The app icon went ink-on-white on 20 September and this is ink on paper. A person taps a #FFFFFF tile and lands on a #FAF9F7 screen, which is a 1.02:1 step and invisible in isolation but sequential here. Both are right on their own terms: a home screen is not a page, and a launch screen is the app's own ground. Worth knowing rather than fixing, unless you would rather the splash matched the icon, in which case the whole first frame stops being offload's paper.

The token the plane needsa mix in light, an existing token in dark
light · --surface-inverse at 90%
dark · --surface-plate, opaque
  • mint --surface-viewer One alias, two scopes: --surface-inverse at 90% in light, --surface-plate opaque in dark. It cannot be --surface-scrim, which is 0.32 and is for a sheet's backdrop: this plane is the surface a picture is shown on, not a dimming of what is behind it.
  • In dark the plane is opaque, and that is the finding. A translucent plane in dark has nowhere to go: --surface-page at 92% over --surface-page measures 1.00:1, so there is no plane at all, only 8% of the thread ghosting through, which reads as a bug rather than as a viewer. Even the darkest available value gets to 1.09:1. So dark takes --surface-plate, #0F0F11, opaque, the same near-black the prism veil uses, and what says a viewer is open is that the screen behind it is gone.
  • Light stays at 90% because there the translucency works: ink over a warm page composites to about #232323, which reads as depth. The two are different on purpose and the alias is what stops that reading as a mistake — hand-mixed in two files, somebody will "fix" the inconsistency and put the dark one back to 1.00:1.
  • The caption and the controls are on #FFFFFF, not --text-inverse. The plane is near-opaque ink in light and an opaque near-black in dark, so ink-inverse would flip to dark text on a dark plane. White is the one value that holds on both.

What this asks for

after the 21 September decisions
  • One token to mint: --surface-viewer, two scopes. Light is a mix, --surface-inverse at 90%; dark is --surface-plate at full opacity, because a translucent plane in dark measures 1.00:1 against the page.
  • One glyph vendored: arrow-up-right, the 82nd, for a link row that leaves the app. Already in assets/icons/ and in the readme's list.
  • One conflict to accept: the splash is two screens, because no platform splash takes a spinner. The seam between them is the thing to get right, and it is the only item here that can look broken on every cold start if it is got wrong.
  • One thing to know: the app icon's #FFFFFF and the splash's #FAF9F7 are one tap apart. Both are right on their own terms, and nothing here proposes changing either.
  • One trade already taken: the people chips left the thread head, so on a shared thread you now tap share to find out who is in. The sheet gives back more than the chips said.
  • Not drawn, and nobody has asked for it: what the holding screen does when /api/me never answers. A spinner that spins forever is the state this design does not have.
