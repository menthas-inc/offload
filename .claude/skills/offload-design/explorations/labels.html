Labels

A thread can hold more than one subject. A trip across Lisbon, Paris and Istanbul keeps all three cities in one flat list today. This page draws labels: named, coloured groups inside a thread that a person makes and applies, and that offload also applies when it tidies a thread overnight. It covers the ten colours, a label slot on every row, the + list for labelling many at once, three ways to filter, where the colour appears outside the thread, and how labels fit with the marks the system already has. Each open question has its recommendation marked.

1. One family of marks

unifying what exists

The system already has six things that work like labels. They split into two shapes, and a label belongs to the first. A mark says something about the row and sits inside it: 24px tall (20 in a dense line), square corners at --radius-xs, a filled ground with white or its own ink on it. A chip is something you press: Chip's 30px pill in a 44px target, neutral fill, ink when selected. A label is drawn as a mark on the row and as a chip in the filter bar, the same way a date is a mark on the row and a preset in the When sheet.

2. Ten colours

OKLCH, measured

Each colour is one solid, used everywhere a label appears: the mark on a row, the pin, the calendar block, the dot. White text sits on every one at 4.6:1 or better, and like moss and blue the solids do not change with the theme. On the dark page they measure about 3.7:1, which clears the 3:1 a shape needs. The hues keep clear of moss (offload did this), blue (this needs you), amber (soon) and clay (late). Iris is the closest to blue and sits 20° purpler; Jade is lighter and far more saturated than moss. A new label takes the next colour in the order below that the thread is not using, so the first three labels in any thread are blue, pink and green, the three furthest apart. The person can change it from the label's sheet.

3. Applying a label

a slot on every row, and the + list for many at once

The thread below is the one used throughout: Europe, October, three cities, and a few things with no label yet.

3A · A label slot on every row chosen tap the labels, or the tag tool on a row with none

Labels follow the date chip on the marks line under the title. A row with none draws a tag tool beside edit. Decided: always drawn on a row with no label, a stated exception to the row's "two controls, never three" rule, because it is the one control that says labels exist.

Tapping either opens the label sheet: the thread's labels with their counts, ticks for this row, and New label at the bottom. Tapping a label mark on a row opens the same sheet with Rename, Colour and Delete for that label.

Many at once · Start from the label chosen the + at the end of the chip bar: one label, many rows

Before any label exists. A thread with six or more things draws the bar holding one chip, + Group with labels, so the feature can be found. Under six, no bar. Once a label exists the chip shrinks to a plain + at the end.

The chip bar ends in a +, pinned to the right edge while the chips scroll under it. It opens New label, and naming the label goes straight to this list: every thing in the thread, grouped as the thread is, with a tick each. Holding an existing chip, or tapping a mark on a row, opens the same list for that label, with Rename, Colour and Delete at the foot.

Done labels all of them in one write with one Undo. This is the answer to sorting a thread after the fact: think of the label once, then tick down the list. It needs no mode, no gesture to learn, and it reads the same on all three surfaces.

Alongside the slot · #label in the composer, and telling offload

Typing # opens the thread's labels, filtered as you type, three at a time, as @ already does for people. Picking one resolves it to the label. A # that matches nothing offers to create one. On a dump from Today, offload files the thread first and then matches the label inside it.

Saying it works too: "put the ferry and the palace under Istanbul". The turn footer shows the thread pill, then the label marks it applied, in the order they happened.

4. Filtering a thread

4A chosen; 4B and 4C kept for the record

Either logic throughout: two labels picked shows everything carrying one or both. A row with no label has its own filter, No label, so a person tidying a thread can find what is left.

4A · A chip bar under the app bar chosen

Drawn once a thread has a label, or has six or more things (then as + Group with labels, see 3). All, then each label with its count, then No label. It scrolls sideways past four.

Lisbon and Paris picked, each chip filled with its own colour. The rows' own marks stay drawn, so the flight reads as both. Every section keeps its heading and shows its narrowed count; a section left empty folds away. The filter is kept per thread per device, and cleared by tapping All.

4B · The thread splits into label sections

Labels become the headings and types become the order inside them. A row with two labels appears under the first and says so. This replaces the type sections rather than sitting beside them, which costs To do's own ordering and the widget layout.

Tapping a heading folds it; folding all but one is the filter. Readable on a thread with three labels, long on one with eight.

4C · A picker in the app bar

A tag tool in the app bar, the third of the three tools the budget allows, opening a sheet of ticks. Quiet until needed, but a filter nobody can see is a filter nobody remembers is on.

With a filter on, the title gains a second line naming it and the tool carries a dot. The strip above the list says how many things are hidden and clears it.

5. The colour outside the thread

Today, maps, calendar, rail

Outside the thread a label is always seen with its thread, because Lisbon on its own could belong to any of them. Colour is the label's solid wherever it appears, with white on it where there are words or a glyph.

Today. The meta line under the title says the thread's name, and the label mark follows it. No label, no mark.

Map. A place with a label takes the solid as its pin; one with two splits the pin between both. A place with none is ink. The chip bar filters the map exactly as it filters the list. Map tiles here are a flat placeholder.

Calendar and rail. Every event is the same neutral block; a labelled one leads with the label's dot, so a week in one city does not turn the whole view one colour. A Google event has no dot, since its own calendar colour belongs to Google. The rail draws up to three dots under a day, one per label with something on it. Rail geometry here is approximate to 015.

6. What offload does

compaction and live filing

offload creates and applies labels in two places: the nightly compaction job, when it judges a thread holds more than one subject, and while filing a dump live, when the dump fits a label the thread already has or names one. No fixed size triggers it. Nothing in the thread announces it: compaction does many things, and a notice for each would be noise. The record is Activity. A person's change is final: a label the person set, removed, renamed or recoloured is never touched by offload again, which is the rule dates already follow.

Activity, as a record rather than a send: hollow lead, what was labelled, the time, and Undo for as long as the row exists (30 days). Tapping the row opens the thread. Anything the person then changes stays as they left it.

7. Android and web

3A with Start from the label, and 4A

Android. Filter chips take Material's 8px radius and 32px height, as the kit's other chips do. The tag tool and the + chip are the same as on iOS; the label-first list is a full-height Material bottom sheet.

Web. The chip bar sits under the pane head, ending in +. A row's tag tool opens the labels as a popover beside it rather than a sheet, and the label-first list opens as a modal. Typing # in the composer works as on the phones.

8. Decided

form, 26 Sep
  • Applying. The slot on every row, the + list for many at once, # in the composer, and telling offload. Suggesting more from a row's sheet, and the eight-second repeat, are dropped.
  • Tag tool. Always drawn on a row with no label, an exception to the two-controls rule.
  • Empty thread. Six or more things and no labels draws + Group with labels. Under six, no bar.
  • Managing. Hold a chip, or tap a mark on a row: both open the label's list with Rename, Colour and Delete. Deleting a label takes it off every row and deletes nothing else.
  • Limits. Ten labels per thread, one per colour. Names up to 24 characters.
  • offload. Nightly and while filing live, with no fixed threshold. Reported in Activity only. A person's change is never overridden.
  • Filtering. Chip bar, either logic, selected chips in the label's colour, kept per thread on this device.
  • Map. A place with two labels gets a split pin.
  • Schema, for the build. A labels table keyed on thread with a name, a colour index and who last set it, and a join table to content and notes carrying the same person-set mark dates use. Sharing's membership rules apply unchanged.
