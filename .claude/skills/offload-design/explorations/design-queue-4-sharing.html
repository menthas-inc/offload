Design queue 4: sharing and the unbuilt

Three questions the codebase raised in August and answered by not building anything. Sharing has a Share button that is inert, no thread can be left, and no row has ever waited for its thread. So unlike parts 1 to 3 there is nothing to show as "in the app now": what follows is the data model's behaviour, which is already decided, and the design that has to catch up with it.

13 · Leaving a shared thread is not deleting it

all · raised 2026-08-27

A thread has members and no owner. Removing a thread other people are in cannot destroy it, since that would delete things you did not author: it removes your membership and your key, and every other member's view is untouched. The thread and its contents go only when the last member leaves. Not built: no client can leave a thread yet

  • A, Leave for a shared thread and Delete for a private one, with the confirmation naming the other members and the toast saying which happened. The word matches the consequence, and the vocabulary table already lives in the readme so a second verb is cheap to add. The last-member case gets the warning and keeps the clay, because it is the only place leaving destroys anything. The cost is two words for one gesture, which somebody has to learn.
  • B, Delete everywhere with the sentence carrying the difference. One verb, one control, nothing new in the vocabulary. The cost is that "Delete" on a thread two people are using is not true, and the product's whole claim is that offload says what it did.
  • The recommendation is A. It follows the rule the readme already states, that offload states the outcome, and the outcome differs.
Recommended: A, Leave and Delete are different words

14 · A row before its thread is settled

all · raised 2026-08-27

Putting something down is instant and the filing happens afterwards, so there is a window where a thing exists and its thread is not final. The data model has no unsorted state: a dump lands in Miscellaneous, the account's default thread, and the sorter moves it out if it belongs somewhere better. So the row's metadata changes shortly after it appears, and the rule is that the row must not reflow when it settles.

What happens now the agent files by thread id, never by name, and there is no waiting state anywhere
Oak brackets, 30cm, matte blackMiscellaneous · just now
Oak brackets, 30cm, matte blackBookshelf build · just now
NOWMiscellaneous, then the real thread, with the change mark
  • The change mark already covers half of this. A row whose thread changes while you are looking at it takes the moss fade, which is exactly "offload did this, you didn't". What is undesigned is the first second, before the sorter has run.
  • Miscellaneous is an ordinary thread. It opens, holds things, and can be renamed, so showing its name is not a lie. It is just often the wrong answer for a second or two.
  • Nothing may reflow. The metadata line has to occupy the same height before and after.

  • A, show Miscellaneous. True, requires nothing, and reflows nothing. The cost is that the commonest first answer is a thread nobody meant, and watching a row say Miscellaneous and then correct itself makes the filing look like a mistake being fixed rather than work being done.
  • B, leave the slot blank. Nothing untrue is ever shown, and the fixed-width placeholder guarantees no reflow. The cost is a row that looks unfinished, and a blank where a name goes reads as a bug rather than as a wait.
  • C, one word. "filing" in moss, in the thread's own slot, replaced by the name when it lands. It says a thing is happening, it says who is doing it through the colour, and it is the only option that reads as progress. The cost is a new pattern: the system has one spinner and no per-row progress vocabulary, so this becomes the first of them.
  • The recommendation is C, and it stays honest because the word is a state rather than a destination. If the sorter turns out to be fast enough that the window is invisible, A costs nothing and this question closes itself.
Recommended: C, with A as the fallback

15 · The share sheet says one thing and should say two

all · raised 2026-08-26

The share sheet states one permission, "they can add to it, they can never delete what is yours", and that is the whole of what a person is told before sharing. It says nothing about what happens to their things in that thread if they later delete their account. What the model does: a shared thread is co-owned and outlives any one member, so deleting an account removes that person's private things and their access, and leaves their things inside the shared thread in place. Not built: the Share control is drawn and inert

  • A, one more sentence. Nine words in offload's own voice, in the place a person is already reading, saying the consequence before they agree to it rather than after they discover it. It is the natural counterpart to the line above it: one sentence about what they can do, one about what happens if they go. The cost is that it does not explain why, and somebody who wants that has nowhere to go.
  • B, explain it properly and link out. Covers the reasoning and gives the Ending things settings page something to point at, which it needs anyway. The cost is length: the share sheet becomes a page of terms before the list of people, and the permission line loses its force by being one of four sentences instead of one of two.
  • The recommendation is A in the sheet and B's paragraph in Settings, on the Ending things page, which currently covers deleting an account without mentioning shared threads at all. That fixes both gaps with one piece of copy in each place.
Decided: B, and the permission line comes out

Applied 5 September 2026. The sheet now reads "A shared thread belongs to everyone in it. If somebody leaves offload, the thread and everything they added stay with the rest of you." and nothing else. The permission is unchanged and still enforced; it is no longer stated here. The Ending things settings page carries the account-deletion half. This reverses the 25 August decision that the permission appears identically everywhere, which is recorded as an amendment on that entry rather than a rewrite of it.
