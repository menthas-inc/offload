Not invited, the invite email, and refusals

The three follow-ons from sign in, first run, landing. All three are decided: signing in without an invite offers the waitlist right there, the invite email is the plain letter with no images, and refusals put a line above the buttons. Second devices are settled too, the tutorial runs once per person and never again, so there is nothing to draw.

1 · Signed in, not invited

decided, waitlist inline

The provider returns a real account, we find no invite for it. Rather than sending the person back to the marketing page, the screen states the situation, shows which address it checked, and puts them on the list in one tap. Using a different account is the quiet secondary, because someone with two Apple accounts will want it.

2 · The invite email

decided, the plain letter

Built the way email has to be built: one 600px table, inline styles, no flexbox, no web fonts, no background images, no rounded corners the layout depends on. Instrument Sans is requested and Helvetica or Arial takes over in every client that ignores it, which is most of them. This is the general template too: the waitlist confirmation, the invite reminder and anything else transactional reuse the same shell.

  • No images anywhere. The wordmark is text, the button is a background-coloured table cell, the rule is a one pixel div. Nothing breaks when images are blocked, which is the default in Outlook and in Gmail's promotions tab, and there is no PNG to maintain at two densities.
  • The shell: wordmark, rule, one headline, one paragraph, one action, the raw URL, footer. Every future message fills the same slots, so the only thing that changes between an invite and a reminder is the copy.
  • The raw URL under the button stays, because clients that strip the styled cell leave the link, and because people forward invites.
  • Every send ships a plain-text alternative with the same subject line.
  • Dark mode. Apple Mail and Outlook invert light backgrounds on their own. Colours are set on both text and background of every cell so the inverted render stays legible, and no near-black text sits on a transparent cell.

3 · Refusals

decided, a line above the buttons

A line above the buttons is the rule. It stays until it is resolved, it sits next to the button it is about, and it needs no new screen. Drawn here on the most common refusal, the provider sheet closing without a token, and applied to the other three cases in the table. A dialog is reserved for the one failure tapping again cannot fix, an expired invite link.
