A second accent beside moss

Five candidates, each judged on the same three things: does it sit comfortably next to moss, does it actually pop against a black-and-white page, and does it survive at 12px on white.

What the second accent is for. Moss already has one job, offload did this, you didn't. A second colour only earns its place if it has an equally narrow job. The obvious one is the opposite: this needs you. Something is waiting, unresolved, or about to expire. That means it will almost always appear as a small badge, a dot on a row, or a count, rarely as a filled button, never as a background. Every swatch below is shown doing exactly that.

1. Ochre
#D97A0A · warm, earthy
green + burnt orange, natural, unforced

The safest complement. Moss and ochre are the two halves of a landscape, so nothing about the pairing feels designed. It reads as attention without alarm, which fits a product that never nags.

My read. Ochre is the one I would lock in. It is the only candidate that pops without raising the temperature of the whole product, it pairs with moss the way two things found outdoors pair, and it stays clearly distinct from the clay you already use for genuine errors. Signal orange is the answer if you want the app to feel more urgent and more branded, but you would need to retire clay and let orange do both jobs. Marigold is really a proposal to merge with your existing amber rather than add a colour. Rhubarb is the boldest and the riskiest. Ink blue is the safe, forgettable one, I would only take it if the accent needs to appear in dense data UI later, where warm colours get tiring.
