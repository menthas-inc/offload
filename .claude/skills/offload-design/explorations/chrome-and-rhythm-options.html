Chrome separation, and a shared page rhythm

Two questions, both about structure. First, how the fixed top and bottom stay distinct from a list scrolling under them. Second, how every tab can open the same way so switching does not jump.

1. Separating the chrome

All six show the same screen mid-scroll. The brand's rule is hairline first, shadow only when something floats, so the options run from the quietest to the most explicit.

1a. What we have now

No border, no tint. Both bars are the same paper as the body, so at rest they read as part of the page and content can appear to touch them.

One thing to weigh: dropping the header also drops its count pill (“Priority 8”, “For now 3”) and the meaning of the first list. On Today the nudge line can carry it (“Six things need you today”); on My things the summary line can (“Six threads, three moved this week”). That is how both mocks above do it, so nothing is lost but a title.

My read. 1c for the chrome: it is the only option that solves the problem at the moment it actually occurs, when text passes under the bar, and it keeps the page white. 1b if you want the most restrained thing that works, 1e if you want the app to feel more designed and are willing to give up the all-white field. I would avoid 1d, since two grey bars sandwiching white is the heaviest chrome in the set.

On the rhythm, yes to both: first section loses its header, later sections keep theirs, and the opening line does the summarising. Say the word and I will fold both into the kit.
