Prism on the phones

Colour for iOS and Android, taken from the marketing site's atmosphere. That effect is a WebGL shader pushed through an SVG turbulence filter, and React Native has neither at a price worth paying, so the same math ran once here and the phones ship the result as a PNG. One texture, decoded at launch, composited with plain alpha. No shader, no blur, no blend mode, no per-frame work, and byte-identical on both platforms. Everything below is drawn at real device size with the real baked assets, so what you see is what would ship.

First, which colours

the same field, mapped two ways

The marketing shader reads its noise through a full-spectrum cosine palette, which is where the oil-on-water look comes from. Faded to 10% that still leans pink and cyan, hues offload does not own. The alternative maps the identical field onto a ring built from the palette we already have: --blue-200, a violet bridging blue and clay, --moss-200, a warm straw, --clay-100. Same shapes, same softness, different vocabulary.

  • Both families are baked deeper than the web washes they come from, and that is forced rather than chosen. The site gets its density from mix-blend-mode: multiply, which React Native does not have: a light tint composited with plain alpha over #FAF9F7 paper is very nearly invisible. Baking the multiply into the texture is how you get the same result without the blend mode, and it is the rule for anything else ported off that page.
  • The mark is baked at full saturation and the atmosphere at 66%, which is the same split the shader makes. Where the colour is the subject it can be a colour; where it sits under content it is pulled back to something that can hold text.

Bthe composer, keyboard down
  • It puts colour exactly where offload works. The composer is the product; the band makes the bottom of every screen the warm end and leaves the reading area alone. Of the four this is the one that reads as deliberate rather than as a texture somebody switched on.
  • The field needs a white bed. The composer's placeholder is --text-tertiary, which is exactly 4.5:1 on flat paper and has no headroom, so the input keeps a 72% white fill and sits on the band rather than in it. Tab labels are --ink-950 and --ink-400 at 10px, and the inactive grey is the thing to watch: measured worst case below.
  • It collides with a decided rule. The plate drops its corners when a conversation is stacked on it, because both carry the same fill; a plate that carries a picture instead has to decide whether the picture continues up behind the raised sheet or stops at the join. Answerable, but it is new work on a part that was just settled.
  • The keyboard case. The plate clears to the composer alone when the keyboard is up, so the band has to shrink with it or it ends up behind the keyboard. Cheap, but it has to be specified.

CMemories
  • Cheapest to adopt of the three that touch the app. One absolutely-positioned image at the top of the screen container. No component changes, no plate rework, no keyboard case, and it composes with the pane head and the app bar without either of them knowing.
  • It is the weakest of the three as a design. A soft shape in the corner is decoration in the plainest sense: it does not mark anything, does not belong to any surface, and could be lifted out with nobody noticing. B at least means something.

D+the same at 30%, to show where it breaks
  • The most colour for the least structure. It reads as tinted paper rather than as an effect, and it needs one line of code: the screen's background becomes an image. No component knows about it.
  • It is the one option that can fail the contrast floor, and the third frame is there to show it. --text-tertiary is #6E6E6E, chosen because it is exactly 4.5:1 on every light surface with nothing spare. Every row's meta line uses it. At 14% the ground is still light enough; at 30% the warm corner takes it under, and that is the whole margin the palette has.
  • It also fights --surface-page. The page is already #FAF9F7, a warm off-white chosen deliberately, and a tint over it means the product's paper is no longer one colour. Rows, sheets and cards are all --surface-raised white and would now sit on a coloured field, which reads as a card lifted off a background rather than paper.
  • If you want it, it wants a hard rule: 14% is the ceiling, --text-tertiary must be re-measured at the densest corner, and it belongs behind the list only, not behind sheets.

iOS and Android, the same picture

option B on both

A baked texture is the strongest parity guarantee available: the same file, the same alpha, the same geometry. There is no shader to differ, no blur radius to interpret and no blend mode that one platform supports and the other approximates. What still differs is what already differs between the two kits: the tab bar's height and label treatment, and the plate's corners.

Answers the asknoyespartlyyes
  • Nothing here animates, and that is the point. The marketing atmosphere costs ~96ms of raster a frame because each wash drifts inside a turbulence filter; frozen, it is one paint. A baked texture takes that to its conclusion: the decode happens once at launch, the composite is a single quad the GPU draws with the rest of the screen, and there is no rAF, no timer and no shader context. It is the same cost as any other image in the app, and it is unaffected by scrolling.
  • The weights above are PNG, and PNG is the wrong format for this. A field of photographic noise is close to incompressible losslessly, which is why these files are what they are; the textures are also baked at 256px because the field is smooth enough that a phone cannot tell it from 512px once it is stretched across the screen. A shipping build should re-encode to WebP, which on this kind of content typically lands well under half the PNG figure. Read the table as an upper bound.
  • Reduced motion needs no special case, because nothing moves. Worth saying out loud since the atmosphere on web has three separate gates for it.
  • Dark theme is not covered by these assets. Every wash in the ring is a light tint chosen against paper, and over #1C1B19 they would read as fog. Dark needs its own bake at lower alpha, or the effect is a light-theme feature and says so.
  • The measurement to take before shipping B or D is --text-tertiary and --ink-400 over the densest pixel of the texture, on both themes. Those two are the only colours in the system with no contrast headroom, and every row's meta line and every inactive tab label uses one of them.
If you want my read
  • B, with A underneath it. The gate gets the full effect because there is no content to protect, and the plate carries a band so the app has colour in the one place that is always on screen and never behind a paragraph. Together they answer the ask without touching the reading surface.
  • C is the fallback if the plate rework is more than you want to spend right now: it is an hour of work and no component changes, and it can be replaced by B later without anything else moving.
  • D I would not ship at a strength high enough to notice. The margin between visible and unreadable is the whole of what --text-tertiary has, and the product's paper is a decision that was already made.
