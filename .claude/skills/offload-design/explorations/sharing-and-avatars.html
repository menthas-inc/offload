Sharing, connections and the prism avatar

A starting point, not a decision. Two halves: how a connection is made, kept and ended, which the product has only ever had in fragments; and a generated prism avatar, so a list of twelve people is scannable when none of them has a photo. Both themes, at real size, on the components as they are.

What the product already has, and what I have not changed: a connection is mutual once they accept, and it outlives the thing you made it for; sharing invites nobody in; and a shared thread belongs to everyone in it, so if somebody leaves offload their things stay with the rest of you. Everything below is built on those three.

1. Adding a connection

existing accounts only

There is no invite, so the only way in is an account that already exists. That has one consequence worth designing around rather than hiding: you have to already know their address.

  • read the kit first The shipped kit assumes invites exist, so the brief breaks it. SettingsDetail.jsx's people page has a Group label="Invites" holding "Priya Raman · Invited on 4 Aug, not accepted · Resend". An invite to somebody who is not on offload cannot be resent, because it was never sendable. That group becomes Pending, and Resend becomes Remove.
  • And blocking is not hypothetical: the same page already ships Row title="Blocked" value="None". So a decline has somewhere to escalate to and I should not have said otherwise.

7. The prism avatar

four algorithms, seeded once, kept forever

Generated on the server from the account id, stored, never regenerated, and the same for everybody who sees them. Drawn here with the real algorithms rather than mocked, so what you pick is what a server can reimplement exactly.

  • One constraint shapes all four: the lightness is locked. Every stop lands in OKLCH L 0.42 to 0.52 at C 0.13, so white initials clear 4.5:1 on every avatar the generator can produce, 4.82:1 at the lightest pixel it draws. The ceiling is measured, not chosen: at L 0.56 the lightest hue in the wheel put white at 4.16:1, which is one person in twelve with a monogram that fails and no way to fix it after the fact. This is the number not to relax later.
  • Hue comes off a 12-step wheel, not a continuous hash. Two people are then never almost-the-same colour, which is the failure mode of every hash-to-hue avatar: at 36px a 15 degree difference is no difference.
  • measured, and it decides section 7 Twelve hues is not enough on its own. The twelve accounts on this page land on eight distinct hues: four of the twelve share a colour with somebody else, which is what twelve buckets and twelve people gives you every time. So the structural axis has to be part of the identity rather than decoration, and an algorithm whose structure disappears at 36px is an algorithm with twelve buckets.
  • The initials stay. A prism can be recognised but not read, and you need to be able to find somebody you have never seen before by name.

The algorithm, written out

for the server
  • Seed: FNV-1a over the account's immutable id, not the email or the display name, both of which can change. h = fnv1a(id), then an xorshift32 stream from it, so every draw below is reproducible anywhere from that one number.
  • Hue: hue = (h % 12) * 30. Twelve buckets, 30 degrees apart.
  • Lightness and chroma are constants, not seeded: L between 0.42 and 0.52, C = 0.13. This is the part that must not be made "more varied" later: it is what guarantees the monogram. 0.56 was the first ceiling and it failed, putting white at 4.16:1 on the lightest hue.
  • Facet angle: (next() * 8 | 0) * 45 degrees. Eight, because a 22.5 degree difference is not one at 36px.
  • Output: one 176px PNG per account, served at 28, 32, 36, 52 and 56, which is every size the product draws. Generated once at signup, stored with the account, never regenerated — a prism that changes is not an identity, and somebody will notice.
  • What it is not: not derived from the name, so it does not change when somebody corrects their spelling, and not derived from anything a person could pick, because the moment it is choosable it stops being a reliable way to find them. Every avatar on this page carries the id it was seeded from in its markup, so the mock does the thing the rule asks for rather than describing it.
  • the honest limit Twelve hues times eight angles is 96 buckets, and that is what makes the difference measurable. On the twelve-person list above the sweep gives 8 distinct avatars out of 12 and the facet gives 12 out of 12. Collisions are not graceful either way: the two are identical, not similar. For friends and family 96 is ample; if a list ever runs to hundreds, the fix is a third axis, not more hues.
  • Nobody has looked at these on a real screen, and a 36px circle at a phone's pixel density is the one thing this page cannot honestly show.
