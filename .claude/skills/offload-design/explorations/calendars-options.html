Calendars: the page, and the thread

Two screens, drawn at 390pt. Neither is new ground. Connected apps already has a Calendar row that taps to nothing, and first run already has a connect screen with the copy for what offload does with a calendar, so the settings page is that row finally leading somewhere and that copy finally having a home. The thread control is the newer thing, and the question it asks is not what it looks like but what a per-thread choice is: the calendars you approved are an account-level permission, and a thread picks from none of them until somebody says otherwise. That is decided in section 5, on safety rather than taste, and it is also what lets the thread's control stay a plain glyph. Every crop is drawn at the governed geometry: AppBar's gutters and group margins, IconButton's 40-in-44, and the badge exactly as IconButton already draws it.

What it asked, before it was answered

the five decisions, for the record
  • 1. The calendar's own colour comes in, as recognition only. Four of seven swatches measure under 3:1 on our light group and two in dark, so the dot helps you find a calendar and the name is what tells two apart. Section 2, with the numbers.
  • 2. A checkbox, not a switch, and the governed one. Every other settings page uses switches, and this is a selection among peers rather than seven independent features. Ink fill, never the moss tick, which this system reserves for a finished row. Section 2.
  • 3. Where new events land is a thread question, not an account one, so the settings page has no row for it. Resolved in section 5: the thread's own selection answers it, and offload asks when the selection cannot.
  • 4. What the thread control says at rest: nothing. Four drawn. Two carry a mark that already means "something new" or invents one outright, and the third is real but leaves nothing to mark. Section 4.
  • 5. A thread picks, from nothing. No calendar is used in a thread until somebody ticks it, and approving one in Settings never widens a thread that already exists. Section 5.

  • The provider colours are the one thing dark cannot restyle, and two of the seven struggle there: on the dark group, which computes to #232323 rather than the --surface-fill I assumed, the purple is 2.23:1 and the grey 2.54:1. The hairline ring is doing more work in dark than in light for exactly those two.
  • Dark is the better of the two grounds here, which is worth knowing: four swatches are under 3:1 in light and two in dark, and they are not the same four. A bright hue falls against #F2F2F2 and a dark one falls against #232323, so no single ground flatters a palette we do not control, and the yellow that measures 1.51:1 in light measures 9.28:1 in dark. Neither is a reason to drop the colour, and both are a reason not to let it carry meaning on its own.
  • There is no "Add events to" row, and that is a decision rather than an omission. offload may write to a calendar as of 8 September and nothing in the product says which one it writes to, so this page looked like the place. It is not: where an event lands is a property of the thing you are working on, so it belongs to the thread. Section 5 is where it resolves.
  • Read only stays on the rows that are, because it is still the honest reason a calendar offload can read is one it cannot add to.
  • Disconnect says what it keeps, not just what it takes. "Threads keep what they already know" is the true part, and it is the part somebody hovering over that row is afraid of.
  • Not drawn: two accounts. The provider block at the top becomes one block per account, and each gets its own list and its own disconnect. Straightforward, and worth seeing before it is built.

4. The control in the thread's title bar

decided: what it says at rest

The bar today is back, then share and the ellipsis, drawn here at AppBar's real geometry: a 20px gutter, --space-4 between the three children, and each side group holding 40px visuals --space-2 apart with a −8 margin so the layout measures the visual and not the 44px hit. The thread bar carries no title, so a third control costs nothing there; on a bar that does carry one it takes the title's measure from 226 to 182 of 390, measured in the crop at 224 and 180 and corrected for its 1px borders, which is the number to check against the longest thread name rather than against the width. What sits under the bar is not just the name: Standing is the thread's name, the people in it as chips, an outline Share chip beside them, offload's standing sentence at 18px and a sparkles caption. That block is the reason section 4 has a fifth option, because it already duplicates a bar control.

5. What a per-thread choice is

decided: B, an explicit pick from nothing

The sheet is Sheet's own geometry throughout: a 24px top, a 36 by 4 handle with --space-6 under it, a 21px title, and --surface-scrim behind. A thread starts with no calendars and you pick the ones it should use. That is the decision, and it is the one that outlives the drawing, so the rejected model is kept below it with what it would have cost.

Where an event landsthe row the settings page does not have
  • With one calendar ticked, the thread has already answered it. Anything offload adds from Lisbon trip goes on "Ana & Jo", because that is the only calendar this thread uses, and no setting anywhere had to say so.
  • With two or more ticked, offload asks, in the conversation, with the thread's own calendars as the values and the existing five-option control doing the asking. It is the case that control was built for, the labels are already short, and it means the answer arrives at the moment it matters instead of being set in advance and forgotten.
  • With none ticked there is nothing to ask, because offload is not reading a calendar for this thread and has nowhere to put an event. Anything with a time on it stays a thing in the thread, which is what it would have been before a calendar was connected at all.
  • Read-only calendars are not offered as an answer, which is the last reason Read only earns its place on the settings row: it is why a calendar this thread reads is one offload cannot add to.
  • What this leaves open: whether offload remembers the answer for the thread or asks every time. My read is that it remembers, as a thread-level default that the same sheet could show, and that is worth drawing before it is built rather than assumed.
