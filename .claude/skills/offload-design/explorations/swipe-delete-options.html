Swipe to delete

Four ways the gesture can work, each wired to a different undo treatment. Every frame is live: drag a row left with the mouse and let go.

Rows come back with the Reset button under each frame.

Areveal, then tap

Drag left and the row parks open over a red Delete. Nothing is gone until you tap it, so the gesture cannot misfire. Dragging another row closes this one.

Deleted

Safest, and the pattern people already know. Two actions to delete one thing.

Bswipe all the way

A short drag springs back. Past a third of the width the row leaves immediately and the undo toast carries the responsibility, with a bar showing how long you have.

Deleted

Fastest for clearing several things. Depends entirely on undo being noticed.

Ctwo actions

The same park, but it reveals Move as well as Delete, since half of what you swipe away belongs in another thread rather than in the bin.

Deleted

Turns a destructive gesture into a sorting one. Needs a wider drag to reach.

Dswipe, undo in place

Commits on a full swipe like B, but the row leaves a slim strip in its own slot instead of a toast at the bottom. Undo is exactly where the thing was.

Survives deleting several in a row, each keeps its own undo. Costs a little height until it collapses.

A is the conservative choice and C is A with somewhere better to put things. If deleting is meant to feel as cheap as dumping, B or D are the honest answers, and D is the one that survives deleting five things quickly.
