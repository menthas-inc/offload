# Rounds and yield in iterated LLM optimisation systems

Research note, 2026-10-10. Scope: how many rounds an LLM-driven optimisation loop needs before returns flatten, what the best systems carry between rounds, and what their stopping rules are. Sources are the papers' own text (arXiv HTML), project blogs and repo configs. Where a number lives only in a figure the text does not restate, that is said and the number is not guessed. Anything from 2023 is marked background.

A reading note on the evidence base: almost none of these papers report "fraction of final gain by round k" in prose. Most plot it and move on. The numbers below are what the text states; the per-round curves are a stated gap in nearly every case.

## How does the gain-versus-generation curve look, and where do the authors say to stop?

### Takeaway
Across evolutionary code search, prompt evolution and harness search, the measured pattern is front-loaded: breakthroughs cluster in early generations, hill-climbing plateaus fast, and sample-efficient systems report reaching the previous state of the art within a small fraction of their budget. Nearly every system stops on a fixed budget rather than on convergence; the only measured convergence rules are EvoPrompt's two-iteration 0.3 percent rule (background, 2023), OpenEvolve's disabled-by-default patience, and HarnessBank's significance-gated "no cell update for P rounds", which is the one with evidence that it matters.

### Cited findings

Harness and scaffold search (2026):
- Meta-Harness (Lee, Nair, Zhang, Lee, Khattab, Finn; March 2026) runs "20 evolution iterations with two candidates per iteration, producing 40 candidate harnesses" on text classification, 40 iterations producing 109 candidates on math retrieval, and 10 iterations on TerminalBench-2; "a typical run evaluates roughly 60 harnesses over 20 iterations". It reaches OpenEvolve's and TTT-Discover's final accuracy "within the first 4 evaluations and continues improving, ending more than 10 points above all baselines". Stopping: "We run evolution for a fixed number of iterations and perform a final test-set evaluation on the Pareto frontier." No convergence rule. [Meta-Harness, arXiv 2603.28052](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28052)
- Meta-Harness's TerminalBench-2 trajectory is a warning about reading early rounds: baseline 64.4 percent, iterations 1 to 6 all regressed (iteration 3 at 63.3 percent), iteration 7 became the best candidate, final 76.4 percent. [Meta-Harness, arXiv 2603.28052](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28052)
- RRSI (Regularized Recursive Self-Improvement of Agent Harnesses, September 2026) runs 26.3 steps per trial against 27.3 to 34.6 for prior methods and 21.2 for the no-evolution baseline, with a cosine-annealed per-round edit budget b_t that falls from b_max to b_min over T rounds so "early rounds can bundle several coordinated changes, while later rounds become sparser and easier to attribute". The round count, b_max, b_min and per-round curves are in an appendix the HTML extract did not include. Terminal-Bench 2.1 rose 74.2 to 80.2 percent; held-out SWE-bench Verified 82.0 to 83.8. [RRSI, arXiv 2609.24972](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24972); headline numbers also in [aiweekly summary](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/rrsi-regularizes-harness-self-improvement-cuts-tokens-30)
- HarnessBank (July 2026) loops "for at most R rounds, or stops after P consecutive rounds with no cell update"; with its paired 2-sigma significance gate the full run stopped at 10.0 rounds on average on TerminalBench-2 (the "10-round floor"), while without the gate runs hit the >20 round cap because "phantom progress" appears in 62 to 76 percent of post-convergence rounds. Gains, vanilla to evolved Pass@1: TB2 36.1 to 45.4, LiveCode 58.1 to 71.8, Omni-MATH 54.3 to 66.0, BrowseComp+ 16.9 to 30.8, GDPval 43.7 to 52.9, AppWorld 41.3 to 56.7, SWE-bench 47.4 to 52.6 (preliminary). The text gives no per-round curve and says "no method climbs monotonically" (Figure 3). [HarnessBank, arXiv 2607.13683](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.13683)
- "Automated Discovery Has No Universally Superior Harness" (Gupta, Lei, Lu, Anumanchipalli, Choshen; July 2026): 30 budget-matched harnesses on 12 model-problem pairs, over 3.1M rollouts. Runs stop at a fixed rollout budget B = N x T; "the text describes no convergence-based stopping rule". Its early-progress result is the closest thing to a measured curve shape: Spearman correlation between best-so-far score and final score is "weak-to-moderate" at 10 percent of budget (0.000 to 0.474) and above 0.70 for 11 of 12 pairs at 50 percent (0.651 for the twelfth). [No universally superior harness, arXiv 2607.18235](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18235)
- AutoDesign (Meta-Harness Optimization for Long-Horizon Agentic Design, August 2026) ran "at least 123 recursive iterations, accumulating 54 harness updates" over 7 days with one candidate per round and a single active harness (no tree search). Figure 1a is described only as "improves the initial harness before reaching a plateau", after which human guidance yields a further gain; no plateau detection or stopping criterion is defined. Average over 7 models on PosterBench-mini: 54.99 to 67.39. [AutoDesign, arXiv 2608.13560](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13560)
- TTT-Discover (Learning to Discover at Test Time, January 2026) runs a fixed 50 training steps of 512 rollouts (25,600 total), no stopping rule, and the text does not say which step produced the best result. [TTT-Discover, arXiv 2601.16175](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16175)

Evolutionary code search:
- AlphaEvolve (June 2025) ablates no evolution, no context, no meta-prompt evolution, no full-file evolution and small-model-only, plotted against compute budget over three runs (Figure 8); the text states only that "each of the components is responsible for a significant improvement in the results". No generation counts for the headline results, no stopping rule; one worked example "needed 15 mutations", and a single evaluation can cost "on the order of 100 compute-hours". [AlphaEvolve, arXiv 2506.13131](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.13131)
- ShinkaEvolve (September 2025): 150 generations for circle packing, 75 for AIME scaffolds, 50 for ALE-Bench, 20 or 30 for the MoE loss (text and table disagree). The circle-packing curve is described as three phases, "rapid early gains, sustained incremental gains, and late convergence via restarts"; hill climbing "shows strong initial performance but plateaus quickly" while weighted parent sampling "maintains steady improvement throughout". It "outperforms AlphaEvolve's solution within less than 150 program evaluations". No stopping criterion. [ShinkaEvolve, arXiv 2509.19349](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19349); the blog says prior systems "often requir[e] thousands of attempts" but gives no counts for AlphaEvolve, OpenEvolve or FunSearch. [Sakana blog](https://sakana.ai/shinka-evolve/)
- OpenEvolve default config: `max_iterations: 100`, `early_stopping_patience: null` (disabled), `convergence_threshold: 0.001`, `early_stopping_metric: combined_score`, `population_size: 1000`, `archive_size: 100`, `num_islands: 5`, `migration_interval: 50`, `cascade_thresholds: 0.5, 0.75, 0.9`. [OpenEvolve default_config.yaml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/codelion/openevolve/main/configs/default_config.yaml)
- Trajectory analysis of AlphaEvolve-style search (Findings of ACL 2026): 15 LLMs, 8 tasks, 30 generations of 10 offspring, two runs each, ~72,000 calls for about $500. Breakthroughs "occur mostly in early generations", with "a strongly negative generation index"; strong optimisers produce "frequent small improvements instead of big and rare breakthroughs". The local refinement rate has coefficient 0.528 (p < 0.001) and lifts R-squared from 0.204 to 0.367. No budget recommendation. [Trajectory analysis, arXiv 2604.19440](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19440)
- DeltaEvolve (February 2026) compares methods "over 100 iterations" and reports efficiency as tokens to best score, for example 2.7297 at 1.39M tokens versus AlphaEvolve-via-OpenEvolve 2.6415 at 1.85M on black-box optimisation. No evaluations-to-target. [DeltaEvolve, arXiv 2602.02919](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02919)
- Darwin Gödel Machine (May 2025): 80 iterations, one new agent per iteration, two in parallel on SWE-bench and four on Polyglot; 20.0 to 50.0 percent on SWE-bench, 14.0 to 38.0 on the Polyglot subset. Fixed budget of 80 iterations, no other stopping rule, per-iteration values only in Figure 2. One SWE-bench run "takes about 2 weeks" at "about USD 22,000". [DGM, arXiv 2505.22954](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22954v3)

Prompt optimisation:
- GEPA (ICLR 2026 oral): total rollouts per task on Qwen3 8B were HotpotQA 6,871, IFBench 3,593, HoVer 7,051, PUPA 2,426, AIME 1,839, LiveBench-Math 1,839. It "matches GRPO's best validation after only 243, 402, 330, 1143, 1179, and 306 rollouts", and counting train-set rollouts only "requires only 79 to 737 rollouts to reach optimal performance". Most of the budget goes to validation, which "is used only for selection, not learning signal". Loop runs until budget B is spent, then returns the best average on the Pareto set. [GEPA, arXiv 2507.19457v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.19457v2)
- DSPy's GEPA: `auto` in light, medium, heavy computed by `auto_budget()` with a minibatch of 35 and 5 full-eval steps; `reflection_minibatch_size` default 3; `max_merge_invocations` 5; no built-in convergence stop, but `stop_callbacks` accept no-improvement and timeout stoppers; no guidance on iteration counts or when returns flatten. [DSPy GEPA overview](https://dspy.ai/current/api/optimizers/GEPA/overview/)
- MIPROv2 (2024): "a budget of 20 to 50 trials with full evaluation on the trainset"; 50 full trials "translates to about 300 minibatch trials". No performance-versus-trial curve in the text; Lesson 5 leaves "different optimization budgets" to future work. Rule of thumb: candidate count N below T/v (trials over variables). [MIPROv2, arXiv 2406.11695](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11695)
- EvoPrompt (2023, background): population 10, 10 iterations on every task; convergence defined as average-score improvement below 0.3 percent for two consecutive iterations. Under that rule SST-5 GA converged after 7 iterations (4.20M tokens) and DE after 11 (6.75M); Subj GA after 15, DE after 17. Curves "gradually converge with an increasing trend". [EvoPrompt, arXiv 2309.08532](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.08532)
- PromptBreeder (2023, background): population 50, "typically 20-30 generations"; "unlike iterative APE, fitness continues to increase throughout the run"; one run shown "over 2000 evaluations". No stopping rule. [PromptBreeder, arXiv 2309.16797](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.16797)

Agentic science:
- AI Scientist v2 (April 2025): four stages with 21, 12, 12, 12 nodes, max debug depth 3, one hour per node, up to 15 hours per paper. Stage stop rules: Stage 1 ends "when a basic working prototype is successfully executed", Stage 2 when runs succeed on at least two datasets and curves converge, Stages 3 and 4 when the budget is exhausted. One of three submitted papers was accepted (scores 6, 6, 7). [AI Scientist v2, arXiv 2504.08066](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.08066)

### Inferences
- The best-attested shape is: a large early step, then noisy incremental gains, then a plateau that is only confirmable by a statistical gate. The three independent pieces of measured evidence for that are GEPA's 79 to 737 train rollouts to optimum out of 1.8k to 7k, the trajectory study's negative generation index on breakthroughs, and the 2607.18235 result that final rank is unreliable at 10 percent of budget and reliable at 50 percent.
- For calibration against the run: round 1 moved warm cost from 1.00 to 0.80 and round 2 from 0.80 to 0.78, so about 91 percent of the measured gain arrived in round 1. That is consistent with the literature's front-loading, and the Meta-Harness TB2 trajectory (six regressions then the best candidate at iteration 7) is the main counterexample worth holding in mind: a flat or negative round is not evidence the search is done when the archive is still growing.
- No system in this set stops on a hand-written "round 2 was small" rule. The ones that stop early do it with a significance test on paired differences (HarnessBank, z >= 1.96 on a sampled subset), because unscreened loops keep "improving" on noise for 62 to 76 percent of post-convergence rounds.

### Gaps
- Per-round gain curves are only in figures for AlphaEvolve (Fig. 8), ShinkaEvolve, DGM (Fig. 2), HarnessBank (Fig. 3), RRSI, AutoDesign (Fig. 1a) and GEPA (Figs. 1, 14, 15); none of the texts restate values, so "fraction of final gain by round k" could not be computed from primary sources.
- FunSearch (December 2023, background) was not fetched; no claim about its curve is made here.
- RRSI's round count and annealing endpoints are in Appendix E.1, not in the readable HTML extract.

## What is carried between rounds, and which carried item mattered most in ablations?

### Takeaway
The ablations agree on a ranking: the selection of what to show the proposer (an archive or elite set, chosen by score and diversity) matters most; raw execution traces and textual feedback matter next; numeric scores alone matter surprisingly little; and novelty for its own sake does not register. Removing the archive or the context selection collapses performance (DGM 50 to 23, DeltaEvolve 2.642 to 1.429); removing scores from the prompt changes almost nothing (DeltaEvolve 2.642 to 2.578).

### Cited findings
- Darwin Gödel Machine ablation (SWE-bench, Polyglot): full DGM 50.0, 38.0; without open-ended exploration (always self-modify the latest agent, no archive) 23.0, 14.0; without self-improve 39.0, 28.0; greedy 39.7, 30.0. Parent selection weights sigmoid(score) times 1/(1 + children), so every valid agent keeps a non-zero chance; the archive is not filtered by score, so "lower-scoring agents can still serve as stepping stones". [DGM, arXiv 2505.22954](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22954v3)
- DeltaEvolve ablation (gpt-5-mini / o3-mini): standard AlphaEvolve-style context versus Blind-Elite (scores hidden) versus Random-Context (no elite selection): black-box optimisation 2.642 / 2.578 / 1.429; hexagon packing 0.972 / 0.970 / 0.786; symbolic regression 3.265 / 3.179 / 2.576; PDE 0.885 / 0.884 / 0.803; convolution 0.897 / 0.911 / 0.550. "Removing scores changes little, while removing elite selection causes the largest drop." [DeltaEvolve, arXiv 2602.02919](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02919)
- Meta-Harness ablation, text classification search-set accuracy (median / best / runs above zero-shot): scores only 34.6 / 41.3 / 26; scores plus LLM summary 34.9 / 38.7 / 23; full method with raw traces 50.0 / 56.7 / 39. The proposer reads a filesystem of every prior candidate's code, scores and traces with grep and cat; on TB2 it touched a median of 82 files per iteration, 41 percent source, 40 percent traces, 6 percent scores. It maintains a Pareto frontier but "imposes no parent-selection rule" and never sees test-set results. Context cost about 10.0 MTok per iteration versus 0.002 to 0.026 for the other optimisers. [Meta-Harness, arXiv 2603.28052](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28052)
- GEPA candidate selection (Qwen3 8B, aggregate over four tasks): baseline 48.84; SelectBestCandidate 54.89; BeamSearch 53.95; Pareto 61.28. Merge of lineages hurt on Qwen3 8B (52.40 versus 54.85) and helped on GPT-4.1 mini (66.36 versus 65.22); the paper says its "budget split and invocation timing need further study". Each candidate accumulates lessons in its own prompt text; no ablation removes reflection. [GEPA, arXiv 2507.19457v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.19457v2)
- ShinkaEvolve: the prompt carries the parent, metrics and textual feedback, top-K and random archive "inspiration" programs, a crossover partner, Reflexion-style parse feedback, and "meta-scratchpad recommendations, appended every T generations". Ablations: embedding-based novelty rejection at 0.95 similarity "provides substantial performance gains over no rejection sampling"; the LLM novelty judge adds only a "marginal" improvement; weighted parent sampling beats hill climbing and best-of-N; the bandit ensemble is "slightly" better than a fixed ensemble in the caption and "significantly" in the takeaways, which conflict. No numbers in the text. [ShinkaEvolve, arXiv 2509.19349](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19349)
- TTT-Discover carries a buffer of every attempt with reward, PUCT over stored states, and model weights. TriMul best runtime, lower is better: full 1203; constant-beta 1484; no entropic 1986; no test-time training (reuse only) 2061; epsilon-greedy reuse 1329; no reuse 5274; best-of-N 5352. Without training "both the mean reward and the max reward stagnates". [TTT-Discover, arXiv 2601.16175](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16175)
- RRSI carries an evolution history of atomic edits (component, hypothesis, diff, delta score, delta cost, accepted flag), a critic that screens diffs for benchmark leakage, a pruner flagging components with no positive measured gain over a window, the best-ever score as a noise floor, and an exploration state that reserves slots for unexercised components when progress stalls. Ablation on Harvey LAB (evolve / in-distribution held-out / OOD / tokens per trial): no evolution 89.4 / 86.9 / 39.7 / 1.56M; unregularised 92.8 / 88.9 / 40.3 / 3.80M; without proposal regularisers 90.7 / 88.8 / 41.9 / 2.69M; without acceptance regularisers 91.5 / 88.7 / 41.0 / 3.59M; RRSI 90.5 / 89.2 / 43.6 / 2.42M. Unregularised evolution scores highest on the evolve set and lowest among evolved variants out of distribution. [RRSI, arXiv 2609.24972](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24972)
- HarnessBank carries a gene bank of cells indexed by where (prompt, knowledge, runtime, config) and why (an inferred failure pathology), each keeping its best harness; parents are chosen by quality-biased selection and the evolver sees the parent's per-task scores, trajectories and metadata. TB2 ablation: full (K=3, 2-sigma) 45.4, zero false elites, 10.0 rounds; without 2-sigma gate same score, +2 false elites, >20 rounds; without confirmation and gate 1.6 lower, +3 false elites, >20 rounds. [HarnessBank, arXiv 2607.13683](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.13683)
- MIPROv2 grounding (program summary, dataset summary, prior instructions with scores, bootstrapped demos, a tip): with versus without, HotPotQA 39.0 vs 36.0, HoVer 32.5 vs 25.7, ScoNe 73.5 vs 76.1. Learned importance is highest for bootstrapped demonstrations and the tip; the dataset summary is high for ScoNe and among the lowest for the other two. Bayesian surrogate over random search: ScoNe 77.4 vs 75.4, HotPotQA 46.2 vs 45.8, HoVer 37.6 vs 37.2, "not statistically significant" on the latter two. [MIPROv2, arXiv 2406.11695](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11695)
- PromptBreeder (2023, background): "Removing any self-referential operator is harmful under nearly all circumstances, the greatest benefit being the initial re-description of task-prompts upon initialization." [PromptBreeder, arXiv 2309.16797](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.16797)
- EvoPrompt (2023, background): selection strategy barely matters (random 47.50, tournament 48.00, roulette 48.17); initial population quality matters less than expected (bottom-10 start 47.80 / 48.64 versus top-10 49.62 / 49.61 for GA / DE on SST-5). [EvoPrompt, arXiv 2309.08532](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.08532)
- Trajectory analysis: novelty coefficients are "close to zero and are not statistically significant"; novelty helps breakthroughs only "when search remains sufficiently localized". [Trajectory analysis, arXiv 2604.19440](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19440)
- AlphaEvolve's prompt carries "multiple previously discovered solutions sampled from the program database", rendered evaluation results, optional explicit context and co-evolved meta prompts; the ablation text reports no numbers. [AlphaEvolve, arXiv 2506.13131](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.13131)
- OpenEvolve's prompt carries `num_top_programs: 3`, `num_diverse_programs: 2`, execution artifacts (`include_artifacts: true`, up to 20 KB) and a "Previous Attempts" section of truncated diffs. [OpenEvolve default_config.yaml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/codelion/openevolve/main/configs/default_config.yaml)

### Inferences
- The two largest measured effects in this set are both about which prior candidates the proposer is shown (DGM archive, DeltaEvolve elite selection), not about how rich the per-candidate description is. The next largest is giving the proposer raw traces rather than scores (Meta-Harness, +15 median points). Scores on their own are nearly inert (DeltaEvolve, Meta-Harness scores-only).
- For the run: keeping the composite as a single parent is the DGM "w/o open-ended exploration" shape, which that paper measured at less than half the gain of archive-based search. The run's follow-ups being "named by round 1's findings" is closer to MIPROv2's history-of-instructions grounding and to RRSI's edit log, both of which have measured positive but modest effects.

### Gaps
- No paper in this set ablates "textual reflection on failures" in isolation; GEPA has no reflection-off arm, and ShinkaEvolve reports no ablation of the meta-scratchpad.
- AlphaEvolve's ablation magnitudes are figure-only.

## Do systems condense findings into a text document between rounds, and how does that compare to carrying raw transcripts or archives?

### Takeaway
Yes, in several forms, and the evidence cuts both ways: a monolithic LLM-written summary loses to raw traces (Meta-Harness) and can collapse the context outright (ACE's 18,282 tokens to 122 in one step), while structured, incremental, per-change notes with the full artifact one hop away (DeltaEvolve's three-level deltas, ACE's delta bullets, RRSI's edit log, AutoDesign's optimisation record) perform at or above raw-code baselines at lower token cost.

### Cited findings
- Meta-Harness tested exactly this on text classification: scores plus an LLM summary (median 34.9, best 38.7, 23 runs above zero-shot) did not beat scores only (34.6, 41.3, 26) and was far behind raw traces searched with grep and cat (50.0, 56.7, 39). The main method carries no summary document; "LLM summaries appear only in an ablation". [Meta-Harness, arXiv 2603.28052](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28052)
- ACE (Agentic Context Engineering, October 2025): when the LLM rewrites the whole accumulated context each step it "tends to compress it into much shorter, less informative summaries"; at step 60 the context was 18,282 tokens at 66.7 accuracy, the next step 122 tokens at 57.1, below the 63.7 no-adaptation baseline. ACE instead carries itemised bullets with IDs and helpful/harmful counters, appended and updated as deltas by non-LLM merge logic with embedding de-duplication. AppWorld, DeepSeek-V3.1 average: offline without reflector or multi-epoch 55.1; without multi-epoch 56.8; full 59.4; online without labels 56.1, with offline warm-up 59.5; ReAct baseline 42.4. Versus GEPA on AppWorld offline: 59.4 vs 46.4, with 357 rollouts versus 1,434 and latency 9,517 s versus 53,898 s. Five reflector rounds and five epochs. [ACE, arXiv 2510.04618](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04618)
- DeltaEvolve stores each node at three levels: L1 delta summary (a FROM/TO strategy line, roughly 20 to 40 tokens), L2 itemised OLD_LOGIC / NEW_LOGIC with a hypothesis, L3 full code. Older elites are shown as L1 only, recent and inspiration nodes as L2, the parent as full code; score change is recorded only as Improved or Degraded. About 36.79 percent fewer tokens than full-code baselines, with a higher best score at lower token cost in the reported example. [DeltaEvolve, arXiv 2602.02919](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02919)
- ShinkaEvolve produces "a search summary document" with "a set of insights outlined in a scratchpad, and recommendations for future solution approaches", appended to the prompt every T generations. Neither the paper text nor the blog reports an ablation of it or how often it runs. [ShinkaEvolve, arXiv 2509.19349](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19349); [Sakana blog](https://sakana.ai/shinka-evolve/)
- AutoDesign carries an optimisation record per iteration: harness, training trajectories and scores, selected component, update plan and code changes, acceptance decision, repository checkpoint. Rejected candidates stay in the record "so later rounds can avoid repeating them". [AutoDesign, arXiv 2608.13560](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13560)
- RRSI carries a log of atomic edits with hypothesis, diff and measured deltas, and the proposer conditions on it; the critic and pruner act on that log. [RRSI, arXiv 2609.24972](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24972)
- MIPROv2's proposer sees a history of previously evaluated instructions with scores plus a dataset summary and program summary; the dataset summary's learned importance is high on one task and among the lowest on two. [MIPROv2, arXiv 2406.11695](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11695)
- AI Scientist v2 does not pass a condensed summary between stages; an LLM evaluator selects the best node and that node seeds the next stage, and nodes keep their own plan, metrics, LLM and VLM feedback. [AI Scientist v2, arXiv 2504.08066](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.08066)
- GEPA's unit of carried knowledge is the candidate prompt itself, which "accumulat[es] high-level lessons derived from observations and LLM feedback"; the Pareto set of candidates is the archive. [GEPA, arXiv 2507.19457v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.19457v2)

### Inferences
- The run's between-round document is human-written by the lead, which none of these papers test; the closest measured analogues are the LLM-written summary (did not help) and the structured edit log (helped modestly and cut tokens). The distinguishing feature of the ones that worked is that each note is tied to one concrete change with its measured delta, and the full artifact remains reachable, rather than a prose synthesis that replaces the evidence.
- ACE's context-collapse result is the specific failure to watch for if an LLM is ever asked to rewrite the findings document between rounds: one rewrite can discard everything and the loss shows up as a score below baseline, not as a gentle decline.

### Gaps
- No paper compares a human-authored between-round brief against archives. Meta-Harness is the only direct summary-versus-traces ablation found, on one task.

## More candidates per round or more rounds, at a fixed evaluation budget?

### Takeaway
The two controlled studies that vary width against depth at a fixed budget find no setting that wins across model-problem pairs; the answer is task dependent, and adaptive branching (AB-MCTS) is the only strategy with the best average rank. Pure sequential refinement is the weakest fixed strategy on hard search tasks (ARC-AGI 8.7 to 10.0 versus 15.0 to 18.6 for repeated sampling), while feedback-driven refinement wins where the evaluator is informative.

### Cited findings
- AB-MCTS (NeurIPS 2025), 128-call budget, Pass@1 and Pass@2, GPT-4o / DeepSeek-V3: LiveCodeBench repeated sampling 37.8 / 40.7, sequential refinement 37.8 / 41.6, standard MCTS 36.7 / 43.2, AB-MCTS-M 38.9 / 43.0; CodeContest repeated 37.9 / 43.2, sequential 30.1 / 41.6, MCTS 37.5 / 43.8, AB-MCTS-M 40.6 / 44.6; ARC-AGI repeated 15.0 / 18.6, sequential 8.7 / 10.0, MCTS 9.0 / 14.0, AB-MCTS-M 12.3 / 16.0. Average rank: AB-MCTS-M 2.3, repeated 3.5, MCTS 4.2, sequential 5.5. On ARC-AGI with DeepSeek-V3 "the performance of AB-MCTS continues to improve substantially as the budget increases from 200 to 500" while repeated sampling plateaus. "Different tasks benefit from different exploration-exploitation trade-offs." Many Table 1 differences fall within standard deviations; MLE-Bench used one run per task. [AB-MCTS, arXiv 2503.04412v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.04412v5)
- "No universally superior harness" varies children per parent N against iterations T with B = N x T fixed: Qwen ladder (16,100), (8,200), (4,400), (2,800), (1,1600); GPT-OSS-20B ladder (4,80), (2,160), (1,320). Result: "No single allocation is best across all model-problem pairs"; on the autocorrelation task the endpoints hurt and intermediate settings sit near baseline. Archive size K in {1, 10, 20, 30} shows "model-problem dependence rather than monotonic improvement with K"; "There is no globally dominant (K, epsilon) setting". On the max statistic the top of the 30-harness leaderboard is K=1, epsilon=20 percent at 0.914; the OpenEvolve regular variants sit at 0.000. [No universally superior harness, arXiv 2607.18235](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18235)
- Test-time scaling literature (reasoning, not optimisation loops) conflicts: "Scaling over Scaling" finds parallel N=32 reaching 53.3 percent on AIME 2024 with a 1.5B model versus 30.0 for sequential at the same budget; "The Sequential Edge" (NeurIPS 2025) finds chains that build on earlier attempts beat parallel self-consistency in 95.6 percent of configurations. The search summary notes that "sequential" means prompted "think more" in the first and feedback-conditioned chains in the second. [Scaling over Scaling, arXiv 2505.20522](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.20522); [The Sequential Edge, arXiv 2511.02309](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.02309v1)
- EvoPrompt (2023, background): population 6 matched population 10 on ASSET at about 2.5x less overhead; total requests scale as N x T x (1 + |D|). [EvoPrompt, arXiv 2309.08532](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.08532)
- DarwinLM (2025, pruning, not prompts): performance improved with offspring per generation but "seems to plateau beyond 24 offspring per generation". Reported via search snippet, not verified against the paper. [DarwinLM, arXiv 2502.07780](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.07780)
- Widths actually used: Meta-Harness 2 candidates per iteration over 20 iterations; DGM 1 per iteration, 2 to 4 in parallel, over 80; AutoDesign 1 per round over 123; TTT-Discover 8 groups of 64 per step over 50; the trajectory study 10 offspring over 30 generations; HarnessBank converged around 10 rounds with K=3 attempts per task. [Meta-Harness](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28052); [DGM](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22954v3); [AutoDesign](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13560); [TTT-Discover](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16175); [Trajectory analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19440); [HarnessBank](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.13683)

### Inferences
- The run's 14-then-10 width over two rounds is wide and shallow by the standards above (Meta-Harness uses 2 per round, DGM 1), but those systems also evaluate far more rounds. The one robust lesson from 2607.18235 and AB-MCTS is that fixing the width in advance is the mistake, not any particular width.
- Sequential refinement's weakness on ARC-AGI and its strength on Nomad2018 maps onto evaluator informativeness: where the eval gives rich per-case feedback, depth pays; where it is a near-binary pass signal, width pays.

### Gaps
- No prompt-evolution or harness-search paper reports a clean "same budget, 2 rounds of 14 versus 7 rounds of 4" comparison; the (N,T) ladder in 2607.18235 is the nearest and gives no winner.

## Adaptive allocation across rounds (start several, prune, reallocate) versus a fixed plan, 2026

### Takeaway
One 2026 study measures this directly and finds every adaptive schedule beats both a fixed single harness and an unpruned portfolio at the same budget, with a three-stage 12 to 5 to 2 to 1 schedule best (85.75 versus 84.54 unpruned and 82.49 single, average over 12 pairs). The supporting mechanism is that rank at 50 percent of budget predicts final rank (Spearman above 0.70 on 11 of 12), while rank at 10 percent does not. Other 2026 systems prune by acceptance gates rather than reallocating budget.

### Cited findings
- "No universally superior harness": start m harnesses, run to a checkpoint at 25, 50 or 75 percent of a full run, rank by best score so far, keep the top s, run survivors to completion, total budget five full-run equivalents. Averages: single harness 82.49; sequential best-of-N 84.35; unpruned portfolio of 5 configs 84.54; adaptive 17 to 1 at 25 percent 84.87; 14 to 2 at 25 percent 85.40; 9 to 1 at 50 percent 85.09; 8 to 2 at 50 percent 85.32; 10 to 6 to 2 at 25/50 percent 85.66; 12 to 5 to 2 to 1 at 25/50/75 percent 85.75, which beats the unpruned portfolio on 11 of 12 pairs; standard error about 0.02. "Partial-run performance becomes increasingly correlated with final performance as search progresses." [No universally superior harness, arXiv 2607.18235](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18235)
- HarnessBank's gates are the pruning: validity (complete ledger), activation (the patch fires at least once), significance (paired mean difference above zero and z = delta / (sigma / sqrt n) >= 1.96 on a sampled subset) before a full training-set evaluation; this produced zero false elites and convergence at 10 rounds versus a hit cap without it. Baseline budgets for GEPA and DGM were 780 to 2,310 rollouts, within 2.1x of HarnessBank's. [HarnessBank, arXiv 2607.13683](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.13683)
- RRSI reallocates within the run rather than across candidates: the per-round edit budget anneals, a pruner removes components with no measured gain over a window, and an exploration state reserves slots for unexercised components when progress stalls over w rounds. [RRSI, arXiv 2609.24972](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24972)
- AutoDesign uses an acceptance gate (training score rises and development score does not fall) with up to K=12 refinement attempts per artifact, and lists "adaptive selection of the next update" as future work. [AutoDesign, arXiv 2608.13560](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13560)
- OpenEvolve's cascade evaluation advances candidates through thresholds 0.5, 0.75 and 0.9, a within-candidate early kill rather than cross-round reallocation. [OpenEvolve default_config.yaml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/codelion/openevolve/main/configs/default_config.yaml)
- AB-MCTS decides per node whether to widen or deepen using Thompson sampling over observed scores, which is adaptive allocation inside a single run. [AB-MCTS, arXiv 2503.04412v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.04412v5)
- AI Scientist v2 allocates by stage with explicit per-stage exit conditions and reseeds each stage from the best node of the previous one. [AI Scientist v2, arXiv 2504.08066](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.08066)

### Inferences
- The run's shape (14 wide, keep 5; 10 wide, keep 4) is a two-stage successive-halving schedule, which 2607.18235 found better than either extreme. Its numbers suggest the pruning point matters less than having one: all six schedules land within 0.9 points of each other and all beat no pruning.
- The HarnessBank result is the most actionable for a $70-capped loop: without a paired significance gate, a loop will report gains on most post-convergence rounds, and the gate is what makes "stop after P rounds without a kept candidate" safe.

### Gaps
- 2607.18235's significance appendix (G) was not in the readable extract.
- No 2026 result compares adaptive allocation across rounds of an eval-driven prompt-tuning loop specifically; the measured case is evolutionary code search on three maths problems with three small open models.
