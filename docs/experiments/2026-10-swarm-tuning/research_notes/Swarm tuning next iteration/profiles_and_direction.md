# Agent profiles, personas and initial direction in LLM swarms

Scope: whether distinct roles, lenses or seed prompts measurably change what a swarm produces; how ideation systems keep the pool diverse; whether an agent allowed to change scope finds different wins; and how the brief anchors the worker. As of 2026-10-10. Evidence quality is flagged per item: most of the 2026 material is preprint and several results come from a single task or a pilot sandbox.

## Key question 1: does persona or role diversity measurably improve quality or novelty versus the same model sampled N times?

### Takeaway
For accuracy on closed tasks the answer is mostly no: personas in system prompts add nothing over a neutral prompt once a no-persona baseline is included, and the model's own sampling variance already gives the ensemble its diversity. For open-ended ideation the answer is a qualified yes: persona and prompt variation widen the idea pool, but the gain depends on which persona (ordinary beats famous, junior beats senior expert), and heterogeneous models move diversity far more than heterogeneous personas on the same model.

### Cited findings
- 162 personas across 4 LLM families and 2,410 factual questions: adding a persona to the system prompt does not improve accuracy over the no-persona control; persona effects were "largely random" and picking the best persona per question performed no better than random selection. Source: [When "A Helpful Assistant" Is Not Really Helpful, Findings of EMNLP 2024](https://preview.aclanthology.org/moar-dois/2024.findings-emnlp.888)
- MARS multi-agent reasoning, reviewer personas (conservative vs aggressive) on GPQA, MMLU and GSM8K across five models: personas lowered accuracy in most GPQA and GSM8K cells (GPQA GPT-4o-mini 48.33 without vs 47.00 with; Llama3.3-70b 60.00 vs 56.67) and raised MMLU only for Mixtral (63.00 to 66.67). Authors' explanation: "the inherent randomness of LLMs already provides a degree of diversity in reviewer feedback" and "aggressive reviewers often generate unnecessarily critical or noisy feedback" when the answer was already right; conclusion: "relying on the model's natural variability might be more effective than enforcing artificial personas". Source: [MARS, arXiv 2509.20502](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.20502v2)
- Solo Performance Prompting (one model simulating several personas in dialogue) beats Standard, CoT and Self-Refine on Trivia Creative Writing, Codenames Collaborative and Logic Grid Puzzle, but the effect "only emerges in GPT-4" and not in GPT-3.5-turbo or Llama2-13b-chat; dynamic fine-grained personas beat a fixed persona set. Source: [Wang et al., arXiv 2307.05300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05300v3)
- MetaGPT role ablation: adding Product Manager, Architect and Project Manager roles beyond the Engineer "consistently improved both revisions and executability, at some extra cost"; removing some roles produced unworkable code. The role ablation is Table 3 of the paper; the separately reported executable-feedback gain is +4.2 Pass@1 on HumanEval and +5.4 on MBPP. Source: [MetaGPT, arXiv 2308.00352](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.00352). Thin: roles here carry different artefacts (PRD, design doc), so the ablation measures pipeline structure as much as persona.
- CAMEL's ablation removes parts of the inception prompt (communication protocol, alignment lines), not roles; no role-free baseline. Source: [CAMEL, arXiv 2303.17760](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.17760)
- Diversity of Thought (DIV-SE): asking the model to ideate distinct approaches, then ensembling, raised GPT-4 few-shot AQuA from 71.90 (CoT) to 84.25 and MATH from 66.46 to 80.76; the diversity is in reasoning approaches, not personas. Source: [Naik et al., arXiv 2310.07088](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07088)
- Diverse-prompt ensemble vs one prompt repeated 12 times (math proof verification, appendix): the diverse ensemble beat both the merged prompt and the best single prompt repeated; repeating one prompt "produces correlated votes" that flip near the decision threshold. Source: [arXiv 2604.02450](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.02450). Dipper (EMNLP 2025) makes the same point for single-model ensembles: self-consistency relies on sampling noise, and varying the reasoning prompt is a cheaper diversity source. Source: [Dipper, EMNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1801.pdf)
- Counter-evidence on pushing diversity: removing the more consistent outputs to raise semantic diversity hurt performance across reasoning tasks (task-dependent trade-off). Source: [Balancing Act, UCL](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10211652/1/8091_Balancing_Act_Diversity_a.pdf)
- Ideation, 1,000 research proposals per setting, DeepSeek-V3 with GPT-5.1 and heterogeneous checks: assigned roles did not prevent diversity collapse. Horizontal junior groups had the highest Vendi score (8.08); interdisciplinary expert groups the lowest (4.65) with only modestly higher quality (8.50 vs 7.88); Leader-Led groups collapsed "almost identically" to no-role groups. Per-agent diversity utilisation (Vendi/N) fell from 1.03 at N=3 to 0.47 at N=7. Source: [Diversity Collapse in Multi-Agent LLM Systems, arXiv 2604.18005](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18005)
- Belief dynamics on networked LLMs: persona prompting changes how an individual agent updates but "does not by itself produce substantial population-level diversity"; heterogeneous underlying models moved the population far more than persona assignment. Source: [Belief Coevolution, arXiv 2607.27512](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.27512)
- Multi-LLM closed loops over 200 to 1,000 rounds show "systematic convergence in semantic representations despite apparent lexical variation"; 13 interventions including decoding, prompt design, agent composition, activation engineering and RL all failed to restore diversity; authors attribute it to autoregressive generation rather than alignment or conformity. Source: [Multi-LLM Systems Exhibit Robust Semantic Collapse, arXiv 2605.17193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17193)
- Human vs LLM ideation: independent LLM samples are less diverse than independent human samples for two reasons, individual fixation (early outputs constrain later ones) and lack of knowledge partitioning (one unified distribution); CoT reduces fixation, ordinary personas beat "creative entrepreneur" personas as sampling cues, and the combination "produces the highest idea diversity, outperforming humans". Source: [Deng, Brucks and Toubia, arXiv 2602.20408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20408)
- GPT-4 product ideation pools were less diverse than human student pools under plausible prompts; CoT prompting gave the highest diversity of any prompt and the most unique ideas. Source: [Prompting Diverse Ideas, Meincke, Mollick and Terwiesch, arXiv 2402.01727](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01727)
- Ten GenAI personas in a plot-ideation study: ideas within one persona were highly similar, across personas average similarity was 0.20. Source: [Wan and Kalman, arXiv 2504.13868](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.13868)
- Multi-agent ideation: enlarging the cohort, deepening interaction and broadening persona heterogeneity each enriched diversity. Source: [arXiv 2507.08350](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2507.08350). Thin: numbers not retrieved.
- Persona-based brainstorming (Doctor vs VR Engineer pairings, separate vs together vs separate-then-together): claims greater depth and cross-domain coverage; abstract reports no numbers and no no-persona baseline. Source: [arXiv 2512.04488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04488). Thin.
- Multi-agent debate amplifies a shared bias ("bias reinforcement") and lacks perspective diversity because all agents share one model; the proposed fix (DReaMAD) diversifies viewpoints by systematically modifying prompts within one model and reports +12.0% accuracy over standard prompting and +20.8% win rate over MAD on MetaNIM Arena. Source: [Understanding Bias Reinforcement in LLM Agents Debate, arXiv 2503.16814](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.16814v2)

### Inferences
- The run's seven fixed lenses are closer to "distinct seed prompts" than to personas, which is the variant the evidence supports: Naik, Dipper and the proof-verification appendix all find that varying the question beats varying the voice. Three lenses converging on the same two changes is what the knowledge-partitioning literature predicts when the lenses are prompts over one model: the partition is in the prompt, not in what the model knows.
- The MARS result maps onto the skeptic stage: a persona that is "aggressive" by instruction produces noise when the candidate is actually good. A skeptic with a concrete disconfirming test is a different thing from a skeptic persona.
- If more diversity is wanted at the ideation stage, the cheapest measured levers are: change the model (not the persona), use junior or ordinary framings rather than senior expert ones, generate independently before any sharing (NGT), and keep groups small (per-agent utilisation halves from 3 to 7 agents).

### Gaps
- No study found that compares a lens-style decomposition (fixed technical angles like caching, prompt, model) against N unconstrained samples at matched cost on an engineering optimisation task. The ideation studies are about research proposals or product ideas.
- The 2026 collapse papers are preprints with LLM-judged quality; human validation is limited (87% agreement on metric orderings in 2604.18005, nothing comparable in 2605.17193).

## Key question 2: how do ideation systems seed, diversify and de-duplicate, and what happens without it?

### Takeaway
Every serious system pairs an embedding duplicate filter with a second mechanism (a feature map, an LLM novelty judge, a tournament or a parent-sampling rule), because raw generation plateaus fast: Si et al. got 200 unique ideas from 4,000 seeds. The one clean ablation (ShinkaEvolve) shows embedding rejection "strongly" beats no rejection and the LLM judge adds only a margin; the AB-MCTS results show that width versus depth is task dependent and should be decided adaptively rather than fixed per round.

### Cited findings
- Si, Yang and Hashimoto: the agent generated 4,000 seed ideas per topic; "We consider an idea as a duplicate if it has a similarity of above 0.8 with any of the previously generated ideas"; "This leaves about 5% non-duplicated ideas out of all the generated seed ideas" and the accumulated non-duplicate count "eventually plateau[s]" at about 200. Best LLM ranker (Claude-3.5 pairwise) reached 53.3% agreement with humans, below the 56.1% human inter-reviewer consistency. Novelty: AI ideas 5.64 vs human 4.84 on a 10-point scale (p<0.01, 100+ NLP researchers blind reviewing). Source: [Can LLMs Generate Novel Research Ideas?, arXiv 2409.04109](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.04109v1)
- Follow-up: ideas rated highly novel at proposal time did not survive execution; novelty ratings are weak predictors of downstream validity. Source: reported via [Autonomous Research Agents survey, arXiv 2608.05179](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.05179). Secondary.
- ShinkaEvolve: mutable code is embedded with text-embedding-3-small and compared against the island's programs; threshold η = 0.95 cosine; above it an LLM judge (gpt-5-nano at temperature 0 for circle packing, gpt-4.1 with up to 3 attempts for AIME and MoE) decides whether the program is "meaningfully different". Parent sampling multiplies a fitness term by a novelty term 1/(1 + offspring count). Ablation (Figure 9, qualitative only): embedding rejection "strongly outperforms" no rejection; the LLM judge adds "marginal improvements"; weighted parent sampling beats both random search and hill climbing. Headline: new state of the art on circle packing in 150 program evaluations. Source: [ShinkaEvolve, arXiv 2509.19349](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19349v1). Flag: no threshold sweep and no numbers for the ablation in the text.
- AlphaEvolve discloses only that its database is "inspired by MAP-Elites and island-based population models"; no island or archive ablation published. CodeEvolve compares CVT-MAP-Elites, grid MAP-Elites and a capped population on circle packing (archive structure, not island count). Source: [CodeEvolve, arXiv 2510.14150](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.14150); OpenEvolve exposes num_islands and migration interval but no ablation is published. Source: [OpenEvolve issue 222](https://github.com/algorithmicsuperintelligence/openevolve/issues/222)
- AB-MCTS at 128 generations: on CodeContest GPT-4o, AB-MCTS-M 40.6 vs repeated sampling 37.9 vs standard MCTS 37.5 vs sequential refinement 30.1; on ARC-AGI GPT-4o, repeated sampling (pure width) 15.0 beats standard MCTS 9.0 and sequential refinement 8.7, and AB-MCTS-A reaches 14.0; average rank AB-MCTS-M 2.3 vs repeated sampling 3.5 vs MCTS 4.2. Authors attribute ARC gains to widening when useful and MLE-Bench gains to exploiting promising branches. Source: [Wider or Deeper?, arXiv 2503.04412](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.04412v5)
- AI Scientist (v1) novelty check queries Semantic Scholar, up to 10 results per query and up to 10 iterations, and assigns novel=True if no clear match. Independent evaluation: it "classified all 10 generated ideas and both seed ideas as novel, despite some being well-documented", attributed to keyword matching. Source: [Evaluating Sakana's AI Scientist, arXiv 2502.14297](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.14297)
- Co-scientist: agents "continuously generate, critique, and refine hypotheses" with a "tournament evolution process"; quality improves as test-time compute scales (abstract gives no figures); Nature 2026. Source: [Towards an AI co-scientist, arXiv 2502.18864](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18864). Secondary coverage reports an Elo auto-rating whose higher values correlated with GPQA Diamond accuracy. Source: [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20250220-google-ai-co-scientist). Thin.
- Station (decentralised research environment): rediscovered 62.7% of oracle sub-findings on average vs 15.4% for Codex Multiagent-v2 and 14.4 to 20.6% for AI Scientist-v2 at matched experiment time; removing Supervisor guidance and Meta Reflection dropped one task from 75.8% to 57.9%; 71.7% of 92 Meta Reflection events were followed by experiments that deepened the existing direction; an oracle author rated 75% of 24 agent proposals 1 or 2 out of 5 for "excessive focus on minor details" (the streetlight effect). Source: [Station, arXiv 2610.08927](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08927)
- Topology matters for the pool: Nominal Group Technique (independent writing before discussion) started with the highest diversity; subgroups sustained constructive conflict; dense debate showed "a rush to agreement". Source: [arXiv 2604.18005](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18005)

### Inferences
- The run had no explicit de-duplication step, so "three lenses proposed the same two changes" was discovered by reading. An embedding filter at 0.8 to 0.95 cosine over hypothesis text, applied before workers are assigned, is the cheapest measured control and would also expose how many of the 24 candidates were distinct.
- Round 2 was pure depth (every pick a follow-up). The AB-MCTS evidence says that is right on some tasks and wrong on others, and that the decision should be made from the round 1 scores rather than fixed in advance: if round 1 found several distinct wins, deepen; if round 1 found little, widen.
- The AI Scientist novelty check is the cautionary case: a novelty judge that always says yes is the same defect as a stub that always says ok.

### Gaps
- No published island or MAP-Elites ablation for AlphaEvolve or OpenEvolve; no ShinkaEvolve threshold sweep.
- Co-scientist's Elo numbers and novelty methodology were not retrieved from the primary paper (157 pages).

## Key question 3: do agents allowed to change scope find wins that implementation-only agents do not, and how is that gated?

### Takeaway
The documented cases of agents changing the problem are nearly all unwanted: extending timeouts, editing scorers, weakening constraints during handoff. There is no measured study of an agent given explicit licence to simplify a feature for cost finding wins the others miss; what exists is the mirror image, which says that unlicensed scope change is common, hard to prompt away, and best gated by hard checks outside the model.

### Cited findings
- Sakana's AI Scientist, when its experiment hit the timeout, "simply tried to modify its own code to extend the timeout period" rather than making the code faster; in another run it edited the code to call itself and looped. Source: [Ars Technica via Slashdot](https://developers.slashdot.org/story/24/08/14/2047250/research-ai-model-unexpectedly-modified-its-own-code-to-extend-runtime)
- METR, o3: reward hacking in 39 of 128 RE-Bench runs (30.4%) vs 8 of 1,087 HCAST runs (0.7%); 21/21 (100%) on Optimize LLM Foundry. Behaviours: overwriting the grader's timer, returning the scorer's reference tensor, monkey-patching evaluators to return success. On Optimize LLM Foundry over 20 runs per prompt: original prompt 80%, "please do not reward hack" 80%, "solve only using methods the task's designer likely intended" 95%, framing it as real Alzheimer's research 70%; METR calls the instruction effect "nearly negligible". Asked afterwards whether its plan matched user intent, o3 said no 10 of 10 times on one example. Recommendation: patch the exploit in the scorer rather than punish the model, and keep checking when blatant hacks stop appearing. Source: [METR, Recent Frontier Models Are Reward Hacking](https://metr.org/blog/2025-06-05-recent-reward-hacking/)
- Constraint weakening across handoff artefacts (1,296 synthetic episodes): normal handoff compression produced 100% blocker deactivation and 54.2% forbidden actions; restoring all four state fields (prerequisite, authority, fallback, execution consequence) brought preservation to 100% and forbidden actions to 0.0%; downstream verification alone removed forbidden actions but left 95.3% of artefacts with the blocker deactivated. Source: [When "Must" Becomes "Maybe", arXiv 2608.24569](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24569). Note: a search snippet attributed "85.2% to 1.6% to 0.0%" to this paper; the fetched abstract does not contain those figures, so treat them as unverified.
- A safety benchmark's test rule: an agent must not "rewrite, expand, replace, or upgrade the original objective" or convert an analytical task into execution "without approval of the goal change"; the safe response is to draft a goal-change request or defer. Source: [ForesightSafety-SAGE, arXiv 2606.08531](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.08531)
- Station gates scope changes structurally: proposals carry a commitment of at least 60 ticks, the Supervisor "generally rejects early pivot requests" except for cases like a missing package, and a new direction after the commitment period "must be approved again". Source: [Station, arXiv 2610.08927](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08927)
- Heterogeneous cohort pilot (20 seeds, 150 steps, custom sandbox): with the Disrupter role removed, discovery of both targets fell from 95% and 100% to 5% and 5%; with the Validator removed, executed safety breaches rose from 0 to 28.6; prompt-only safety produced 10.1 breaches. The paper's claim that a lone agent "would self-censor" high-entropy proposals is motivation, not measured. Source: [Heterogeneous Agent Cohorts, arXiv 2607.11226](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11226v1). Thin: pilot sandbox, single lab.
- Anthropic's lead agent brief: "Each subagent needs an objective, an output format, guidance on the tools and sources to use, and clear task boundaries"; without them "agents duplicate work, leave gaps, or fail to find necessary information"; multi-agent (Opus 4 lead, Sonnet 4 subagents) beat single Opus 4 by 90.2% on an internal breadth-first research eval; token usage alone explained about 80% of performance variance on BrowseComp; evaluate end state rather than process. Source: [Anthropic, multi-agent research system](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)

### Inferences
- The question the run did not ask ("should a product feature change?") is the one the literature treats as a governance boundary, not an optimisation lever. If it is added, the measured pattern is a separate proposer role whose output is a goal-change request that a person approves, with a hard check (not a prompt line) that implementation workers cannot act on it. METR's numbers say the prompt line alone does nothing.
- The Disrupter result is the only measured hint that a role explicitly licensed to propose out-of-distribution changes finds things the others cannot, and it is a pilot. It is consistent with, not proof of, the value of a scope-change proposer.
- Offload's own constraint applies here: the privacy policy is a claim surface, so a scope change that touches retention, recipients or logging is a "tell me before writing the code" event by the project's own rules, which is exactly the gate the benchmark rule describes.

### Gaps
- No study measured "scope-change agent finds X% more savings than implementation-only agents" on a cost-optimisation task. The closest material is reward hacking (unwanted scope change) and the Disrupter pilot (wanted, in a toy sandbox).
- No source uses the term "non-goals" for agent briefs; Anthropic's "clear task boundaries" is the nearest documented practice.

## Key question 4: does the brief anchor the worker, and what reduces it?

### Takeaway
Yes, and robustly: LLMs reinforce premises embedded in the prompt, favour early elements, carry their prior belief through both the reasoning and the answer, and anchor on scores supplied as metadata even when told to ignore them. Chain of thought and "disregard" warnings do not remove the effect; what works in the measured cases is structural, a separate evaluator without the anchor, evidence sampled independently of the initial hypothesis, and state fields that make the disconfirming condition explicit.

### Cited findings
- Confirmation bias in CoT (Findings of ACL 2025): the model's prior belief, measured as its direct-answer probability, "not only skew[s] the reasoning process but also affect[s] the final answer", biasing both rationale generation and how the rationale is used; the uneven effect of CoT across tasks tracks each task's vulnerability to confirmation bias. Source: [Unveiling Confirmation Bias in Chain-of-Thought Reasoning, arXiv 2506.12301](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.12301v1)
- LLMs "reinforce the premises embedded in user queries (confirmation bias)" and "favor initial or prominent elements within a prompt (position bias)". Source: [Confirmation, Framing, and Position Biases in LLM Responses, ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3786304.3787879)
- Heuristic-inducing prompt structures: anchoring observed "at rates significantly above chance"; debiasing interventions reduced representativeness heuristics but "exerted limited impact on anchoring and framing". Source: [Vulnerability of LLM Outputs to Heuristics-Inducing Prompt Structures, ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3742413.3789108)
- LLM-as-judge anchoring (185,271 evaluations, 8 models): prior scores passed as context metadata "systematically shift ratings toward their values"; largest Cohen's d 0.71; on human-labelled industry data anchored metadata blocked 48% of error corrections and flipped 10.18% of correct judgments; CoT did not reduce the total effect and a metadata-disregard warning did not either. Source: [Anchoring Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge Systems, arXiv 2608.25869](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25869)
- Anchoring is a robustness failure, not a knowledge failure: models resist anchors when confidently wrong and remain susceptible when accurate but low-confidence. Source: [Localizing Anchoring Pathways, arXiv 2606.12818](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.12818)
- Pre-registered 2-4-6 rule-discovery study with LLM chatbots: the unmodified model reduced discovery and inflated confidence about as much as explicitly sycophantic prompting; unbiased sampling from the true distribution gave discovery rates about five times higher; mechanism: "when the evidence is selected based only on an initial piece of data, the agent becomes more confident in a wrong hypothesis without getting closer to the truth". Source: [A Rational Analysis of the Effects of Sycophantic AI, arXiv 2602.14270](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14270)
- Sycophancy in agents reaches planning: an agent "may filter tool outputs to fit the user's view"; a red-team or contrarian stance at inference time is suggested as a counter. Source: [Agentic RL survey, arXiv 2509.02547](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.02547)
- Debate reinforces a shared bias rather than correcting it when agents share one model. Source: [arXiv 2503.16814](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.16814v2); a 2026 ICML poster finds biased consensus emerging in multi-agent debate with sampling noise as a driver. Source: [Emergence of Biased Consensus, ICML 2026](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/66269)
- Constraint fields that survive handoff (prerequisite, authority, fallback, consequence) took forbidden actions from 54.2% to 0.0%; a downstream verifier alone removed the forbidden actions without the executor itself recognising the constraint. Source: [arXiv 2608.24569](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24569)
- Anthropic: vague briefs caused subagents to "misread tasks or repeated each other's searches"; the fix was objective, output format, tools and boundaries, plus effort rules embedded in the prompt (simple fact: 1 agent, 3 to 10 calls; comparison: 2 to 4 subagents, 10 to 15 calls each) because "agents struggle to judge appropriate effort". Source: [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)
- Station's Supervisor and Meta Reflection raised persistence (project duration 55.5 to 67.0 ticks) and rediscovery, but 71.7% of reflections deepened the existing direction, so reflection mostly confirmed the handed direction rather than redirecting. Source: [Station, arXiv 2610.08927](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08927)

### Inferences
- "A lever that does not help is a finding" is a disregard warning, and the judge-anchoring paper measured that class of instruction as ineffective. Workers keeping 21 of 24 while skeptics kept 9 is the expected gap between the anchored party and a separate evaluator; the skeptic count is the better estimate of what actually worked.
- The briefing techniques with measured support are structural: (a) the worker's brief names the disconfirming test and the threshold before the work starts (the pre-registration analogue of the 2-4-6 study's unbiased sampling); (b) the verifier does not see the worker's recommendation or the lens that produced the hypothesis (the metadata anchor); (c) the "keep" decision is made from the measured delta against the pre-stated threshold, not from the worker's prose; (d) hypothesis order in a shared brief is randomised or each worker sees only its own, since position bias favours early items.
- Telling round 2 lenses what round 1 found is a shared anchor by construction. The Station result says guidance raises persistence, which is what was observed (fast convergence), and the collapse papers say it narrows the pool, which is the suspected cost. A split round (half the lenses briefed on round 1, half blind) would measure that directly at no extra method cost.

### Gaps
- No study found that tests "ask for the null result up front" or pre-registration as a prompt intervention on LLM agents specifically; the 2-4-6 study is about humans using LLMs.
- No study measures confirmation of a handed hypothesis in a coding or optimisation worker; the evidence is from QA, judging and rule discovery.

## Key question 5: does a wildcard or open-brief agent beside the lens agents find different things?

### Takeaway
Thin evidence, pointing the same way: a role licensed to propose unconventional moves recovers targets the structured roles miss (one pilot), decentralised agents choosing their own directions rediscover far more than pipeline systems (one preprint), and the same decentralised agents drift to easy targets without a supervisor. Nobody has run "N lenses plus one wildcard" against "N+1 lenses".

### Cited findings
- Removing the Disrupter role dropped target discovery from 95% and 100% to 5% and 5% across 20 seeds, attributed to the cohort being unable to cross a barrier the structured roles would not attempt; homogeneous debate (M7) found 10% at 121,508 tokens per success vs 72,180 for the full cohort. Source: [arXiv 2607.11226](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11226v1)
- Station agents "independently propose and pursue their own directions" and rediscovered 62.7% vs 15.4% and 14.4 to 20.6% for pipeline or root-delegation systems; without supervision they drift to easy targets ("optimizing memory performance in the RNN task"). Source: [arXiv 2610.08927](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08927)
- Frontier agents on open-ended AI research (five runs): weak experimental design, flawed inference, "poor adaptation to negative results", inefficient resource use; they can do the engineering but struggle with the research lifecycle. Source: [Can AI agents conduct open-ended AI research?, arXiv 2607.27191](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.27191)
- Junior-persona horizontal groups were the most diverse (Vendi 8.08) while interdisciplinary experts were the least (4.65); NGT (independent first) kept the most diversity. Source: [arXiv 2604.18005](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18005)
- Ordinary personas beat celebrity creative personas as sampling cues and CoT counters fixation. Source: [arXiv 2602.20408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20408)

### Inferences
- A wildcard here should be an open brief on the same measurement harness, not a persona: "find the cheapest change to this turn, any layer, with this budget and these non-goals", run blind to the lens list and to round 1, with the same de-duplication filter applied after. The measured risk is the streetlight effect, so its proposals need the same skeptic pass and a cost floor below which a finding does not count.
- The cheapest version of the experiment is to keep the seven lenses, add one open brief and one "junior" rephrasing of the open brief, and count how many of their candidates survive de-duplication against the lens pool. That number is the direct answer to this question for this codebase.

### Gaps
- No controlled comparison of a wildcard beside specialised agents exists; both supporting results are from a toy sandbox and a single research-environment preprint.
