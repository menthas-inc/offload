# Communication topologies and shared state in LLM agent swarms

Research note, as of 2026-10-10. Every claim carries a link. "Vendor" marks a result reported by the party that built the thing. "Preprint" marks an arXiv paper with no visible peer review. Where a paper's numbers sit only in a figure the text does not restate, that is said and nothing is estimated.

A short orientation to the shapes compared below, so the sections can refer to them by name:

- **Hub and spoke**: a lead decomposes, workers run in isolation, results return to the lead. Anthropic's research system, Claude Code subagents, OpenAI agents-as-tools.
- **Blackboard or shared memory**: workers read and write one shared store mid-task. MetaGPT's message pool, LbMAS, AutoGen group chat (a shared thread), LangGraph shared state.
- **Debate and critique**: agents see each other's answers and revise. Du et al. style debate, Mixture of Agents layers.
- **Meta-review loop**: critiques from one round are condensed and fed to the next round's generators. Google's AI co-scientist, and the run under critique.
- **Tournament with Elo**: pairwise judged comparisons rank candidates. AI co-scientist's ranking agent.
- **Hierarchical team with managers**: ChatDev's CEO/CTO chain, MetaGPT roles.
- **Peer to peer**: workers message each other directly. Claude Code agent teams, AgentRoom's broadcast log.

## What the named systems and the 2026 papers measure about communication structure

### Takeaway
Measured results favour a lead with isolated workers for breadth-first research, structured roles over free dialogue in engineering pipelines, and topology changes over prompt changes when fixing failures; graph or mesh topologies win on research-style benchmarks but adding agents past three lowers overall score. Almost no paper ablates the communication channel itself in isolation, so most "topology" evidence is a whole-system comparison.

### Cited Findings
- Anthropic's research system (vendor): an Opus 4 lead with Sonnet 4 subagents "outperformed single-agent Claude Opus 4 by 90.2% on our internal research eval"; on BrowseComp "token usage by itself explains 80% of the variance", with tool calls and model choice the other two of three factors that explain 95%; multi-agent "use about 15× more tokens than chats"; parallel subagents cut research time "by up to 90% for complex queries", [Anthropic engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)
- Same source, observed coordination failures: early agents spawned "50 subagents for simple queries", agents "distracting each other with excessive updates", and vague delegation caused duplication (one subagent explored the 2021 chip crisis while two others repeated work on 2025 supply chains). Synchronous execution means the lead cannot steer subagents and subagents cannot coordinate with each other. Domains "needing shared context or many inter-agent dependencies, such as most coding tasks, are a poor fit today", [Anthropic engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)
- MAST (Berkeley, NeurIPS 2025): 14 failure modes from 1,600+ annotated traces across 7 frameworks, taxonomy built from 150 traces with inter-annotator kappa 0.88. Overall shares: step repetition 15.7%, reasoning-action mismatch 13.2%, unaware of termination conditions 12.4%, disobey task specification 11.8%, incorrect verification 9.10%, no or incomplete verification 8.20%, task derailment 7.40%, fail to ask for clarification 6.80%, premature termination 6.20%, loss of conversation history 2.80%, conversation reset 2.20%, ignored other agent's input 1.90%, disobey role specification 1.5%, information withholding 0.85%. Summing the modes gives roughly 44% system design, 32% inter-agent misalignment, 24% task verification (the paper states the modes, not the category totals), [arXiv 2503.13657v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v3)
- MAST intervention case studies (Table 5, accuracy %): AG2 GSM-Plus GPT-4 baseline 84.75 ± 1.94, improved prompt 89.75 ± 1.44, new topology (Problem Solver, Coder, Verifier, only the Verifier can end) 85.50 ± 1.18, topology gain not significant (Wilcoxon p = 0.4); with GPT-4o baseline 84.25, prompt 89.00, topology 88.83 (p = 0.03 for both). ChatDev ProgramDev-v0 baseline 25.0, prompt 34.4, new cyclic topology (ends only when CTO confirms) 40.6; HumanEval 89.6, 90.3, 91.5. Authors: "topology-based changes are more effective than prompt-based changes for both systems" but the interventions "do not constitute substantial improvements", [arXiv 2503.13657v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v3)
- MAST on communication fixes: "Solutions focused on context or communication protocols are often insufficient for FC2 failures" (inter-agent misalignment), which the authors attribute to a need for social reasoning; verification is "superficial" in many frameworks and "Multi-Level Verification is Needed", [arXiv 2503.13657v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v3)
- MultiAgentBench / MARBLE (research scenario): graph topology "excels in research scenarios with the best task performance", star is similar on task score, tree "performs worst" with the lowest task and coordination scores and high token use; group discussion as a planning strategy "scores the worst across all metrics"; an ablation at 1, 3, 5 and 7 agents found adding agents lowers the overall KPI, with coordination score rising sharply from 1 to 3 agents and task score rising only gradually. Exact scores sit only in Figures 5 and 6, [arXiv 2503.01935v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01935v1)
- Google AI co-scientist (vendor, Nature revision): hypotheses start at Elo 1200; top-ranked hypotheses meet in multi-turn "scientific debates", lower-ranked ones get single-turn pairwise comparisons; matches are drawn by the proximity graph (similar hypotheses) and recency. The meta-review agent summarises recurring patterns from reviews and tournament debates into a critique appended to the Reflection and Generation agents' prompts next iteration, with no fine-tuning; the Generation agent uses it "selectively to avoid overfitting". Taking each GPQA diamond question's top-Elo result gives top-1 accuracy 78.4%; across 203 research goals best Elo and top-10 Elo trend upward with compute, and on 15 expert-curated goals the authors "observed no performance saturation", [arXiv 2502.18864v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18864v1)
- Co-scientist's own caveat: "Note that the Elo metric is auto-evaluated and not based on independent ground truth", and it may favour attributes that do not match scientists' preferences; expert evaluation covered 11 goals and is subjective, [arXiv 2502.18864v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18864v1)
- Sakana Multi-LLM AB-MCTS (vendor): on 120 ARC-AGI-2 public tasks with a 250-call cap, repeated sampling with o4-mini solved 23%, AB-MCTS with o4-mini 27.5%, Multi-LLM AB-MCTS (o4-mini, Gemini 2.5 Pro, R1-0528) "over 30%" at Pass@250; the gap opens "after about 50 LLM calls". Each model gets its own Thompson-sampled quality model so the search shifts calls to whichever model is working on that problem. Caveat from Sakana: with rule-based selection of two final answers, Pass@2 is 19.2%, a gap of over 10 points, so finding a correct answer and choosing it are different problems, [Sakana AB-MCTS](https://sakana.ai/ab-mcts/)
- Sakana ShinkaEvolve (vendor): an archive of evaluated programs with parent sampling balancing exploit and explore, a novelty rejection filter (embedding similarity plus an LLM novelty judge) that screens proposals "to avoid spending time on minor, uninteresting variations", and a bandit over an LLM ensemble. Reported: circle packing state of the art in 150 samples, ALE-Bench AHC015 817,371 vs 762,641 for ALE-Agent, an MoE load-balancing loss that cut inefficient routing 5.81% and improved average task performance 1.73% across seven benchmarks, [Sakana ShinkaEvolve](https://sakana.ai/shinka-evolve/)
- Independent comparison on engineering tasks (preprint): Frontier-Eng ran ShinkaEvolve and AB-MCTS with claude-opus-4.6 and gpt-oss-120b; ShinkaEvolve showed "more sustained late-stage updates" and claude-opus-4.6 under ShinkaEvolve retained a median gain of 72.9%, [arXiv 2604.12290](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.12290)
- Mixture of Agents (ICLR 2025): with a Qwen1.5-110B aggregator and 2 layers, six distinct proposers scored 61.3% against 56.7% for a single proposer sampled six times; at n=3, 58.0 vs 56.1; at n=2, 58.8 vs 54.5. The aggregator ablation argues it is "sophisticatedly synthesizing rather than just picking and selecting", [arXiv 2406.04692](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.04692); a follow-up healthcare summarisation system found a second layer helped (0.3590 to 0.4376 multi-proposer) and a third hurt (0.4050), read by the authors as over-correction, [arXiv 2504.03932](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.03932)
- ChatDev ablation (vendor): removing the communicative dehallucination step dropped every metric, and removing roles from system prompts had the largest effect, [arXiv 2307.07924](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.07924). MetaGPT uses a global message pool that agents publish structured messages to and subscribe from, and contrasts documents and diagrams with ChatDev's dialogue; its 85.9% HumanEval Pass@1 is a whole-system number with no pool ablation in what I could read, [MetaGPT reference summary](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/aiwg@2026.7.12/docs/references/REF-013-metagpt-multi-agent-framework.md). AgileCoder notes the global pool's cost: "direct exposure of all data to the agents could lead to information overload", [arXiv 2406.11912](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.11912)
- AgentVerse: on some benchmarks "the Group setup does not show a clear advantage over the Solo setup for both LLMs", [search summary of arXiv 2308.10848](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.10848). The PDF would not extract; see Gaps.
- 2026 topology learning: GoAgent reports 93.84% average accuracy and about 17% fewer tokens across six benchmarks by generating task-specific topologies over groups of agents, [arXiv 2603.19677](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19677v1); a causal-inference paper discovers "efficient and explainable" topologies, [arXiv 2608.12921](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.12921); TeamBench runs 8 to 512 agents under chain, star, tree, hierarchical, fully connected, sparse mesh and dynamic reputation topologies, [search result](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/394298482_MultiAgentBench_Evaluating_the_Collaboration_and_Competition_of_LLM_agents). These are search-level findings; I did not open the papers.
- Relay stages without new signal: a Medium field guide reports gpt-4.1-mini accuracy falling from 90.7% with one stage to 41.2% with two in a controlled test. Secondary source, unverified, [Medium](https://medium.com/@Micheal-Lanham/multi-agent-in-production-in-2026-what-actually-survived-f86de8bb1cd1)

### Inferences
- The evidence that favours hub and spoke is about breadth-first retrieval, not engineering. Anthropic says so themselves (coding is "a poor fit today"), and MAST's largest coding gains came from adding a verifier role with the right to end the conversation, which is a structural change inside a pipeline, not a topology change between peers.
- Across MAST, MultiAgentBench and the co-scientist, the recurring wins are (a) a role whose only job is to refuse, (b) a condensed critique that the next round reads, and (c) a search that keeps diversity explicitly (proximity graph, novelty filter). None of them come from free peer messaging.
- The run under critique already matches the co-scientist's shape (generate, reflect, rank, meta-review into the next round) minus the tournament. The co-scientist's own caveat applies: an Elo computed by the same model family is a self-rating, and the paper's accuracy evidence is one top-1 number on GPQA plus small expert panels.

### Gaps
- The co-scientist ablation table (each agent disabled, Elo and GPQA deltas) is in a supplementary table the fetched HTML did not include. The design is described; the numbers are not.
- MultiAgentBench's per-topology scores are in figures only.
- AgentVerse's horizontal versus vertical communication numbers: the PDF did not extract and I did not find a text version.
- No paper I found ablates MetaGPT's shared message pool on its own.

## Does a skeptic or critic stage improve the final result, and how is critic calibration measured

### Takeaway
A dedicated verifier role measurably improves pipelines (MAST's ChatDev and AG2 cases), but LLM critics over-reject badly, and the one study that measured it found false rejection rates from 25% to 88% depending on model and prompt, with richer prompts making it worse. Calibration is measured as a false negative rate on a labelled correct/buggy pair set, and the only fix with numbers grounds the verdict in an executed test.

### Cited Findings
- MAST: adding a Verifier role that alone can end the conversation raised AG2 GSM-Plus from 84.25 to 88.83 on GPT-4o (p = 0.03); giving ChatDev's CEO the final say gave +9.4% overall, and a high-level task objective verification step gave +15.6% on ProgramDev; ChatDev ProgramDev-v0 went 25.0 to 40.6 with the cyclic review topology, [arXiv 2503.13657v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v3)
- Over-correction study (preprint, 700+ tasks paired into 1400+ correct/buggy instances on HumanEval-X-Bugs, a buggy MBPP variant, and QuixBugs; GPT-4o, Claude-4.5-sonnet, Gemini-2.0-flash, Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-Small-3.1-24B): false rejection rate (rejecting correct code) under Direct / Direct+Explain / Full prompting on MBPP was GPT-4o 35.9 / 74.1 / 87.9, Claude-4.5-sonnet 58.5 / 55.7 / 62.3, Gemini-2.0-flash 34.7 / 35.1 / 39.6, Llama-3.1-8B 74.7 / 91.9 / 88.2; on HumanEval GPT-4o went 26.2 / 58.5 / 73.2. "Richer prompts mainly shift the decision boundary toward rejection: FPR tends to fall while FNR rises sharply." Four rationale categories cover 87.2% of false rejections: logic error 48.2% (n=2018), added requirement 14.1%, boundary error 13.2%, misread spec 11.7%, [arXiv 2603.00539](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00539)
- Same study, the fix: a "Fix-guided Verification Filter" runs when the critic says NO and proposes a fix, executes original and fixed code against benchmark tests plus GPT-4o spec-constrained tests, and flips to YES when the fix is behaviourally equivalent or breaks passing code. Under Full prompting average FNR fell 54.8% to 16.3% on HumanEval, 69.0% to 28.9% on MBPP, 51.0% to 24.0% on QuixBugs; Llama-3.1-8B on MBPP 90.81% to 23.56%. FPR rose modestly (GPT-4o on HumanEval 0.00% to 1.83%). Authors: "LLMs are not reliably calibrated for requirement-conformance judgment without running tests" and "explanations are often inconsistent with verdicts", [arXiv 2603.00539](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00539)
- Verify-repair loops (preprint): models false-acceptance and false-rejection rates of verifiers inside agent loops and finds "the bias direction differs across verifier families", so it should not be treated as a fixed correction, [arXiv 2607.17641](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17641v1)
- LLM-built verifiers for code share biases: LLM-written test verifiers miss many human-written bugs and "LLM errors cluster tightly, suggesting shared systematic biases", [arXiv 2507.06920](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.06920)
- Weak verifiers in repeated sampling "often show poor calibration and high false positive rates" (the false acceptance direction), [Weaver, Stanford](https://scalingintelligence.stanford.edu/pubs/weaver.pdf)
- ExecuCritic (preprint): a critic trained on execution rollouts predicts pass/fail, and the coder uses its feedback only when the critic agrees with the sandbox executor, a gating design that exists because critic verdicts were not trusted on their own, [arXiv 2609.16604](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16604v1)
- AgentAuditor (preprint): replaces majority voting with a search over a reasoning tree of agreements and divergences, trained to favour correct minority answers, and reports beating majority vote and LLM-as-judge, [arXiv 2602.09341](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09341v1)
- Framework-level: Claude Code's docs recommend adversarial teammates for debugging ("Have them talk to each other to try to disprove each other's theories") on the argument that sequential investigation "suffers from anchoring"; this is a design rationale with no measurement attached, [Claude Code agent teams](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-teams)

### Inferences
- The run's skeptics keeping 9 of 24 where workers wanted 21 of 24 is consistent with the literature in both directions: workers over-accept (Weaver's high false positive direction), critics over-reject (the 2603.00539 FNRs). The reported fact that every refusal was reproducible and several named the flip condition is exactly the "fix-guided" signal that study found separates a real rejection from an over-correction, so the run's skeptic design already sits on the right side of that paper.
- Calibration cannot be read off acceptance rate. The measurement that exists is a labelled pair set: known-good and known-bad candidates fed through the critic, reporting FNR and FPR separately. For a swarm, the cheap version is seeding each round with one or two candidates whose verdict is known.
- "Richer prompts shift the boundary toward rejection" is a warning for a skeptic brief: asking the critic to explain and to fix raises false refusals unless the explanation is checked by execution.

### Gaps
- No study I found measures a critic stage's effect on the final composed result of an optimisation run, as opposed to per-candidate accept/reject accuracy. The MAST verifier cases are the closest and they measure task accuracy of the whole pipeline.
- No measurement of critic calibration on fuzzy engineering candidates (prompt or model changes judged against an eval score) rather than code with a test oracle.

## Workers reading each other's interim findings during a round versus condensed findings between rounds

### Takeaway
Direct measurements are new and mostly about code: isolated parallel workers interfere semantically at high rates on constructed tasks, a short message describing a completed change removes almost all of it, concurrent writes are the largest failure class in a 2,124-run study, and Anthropic observed both duplication (from vague briefs) and distraction (from excessive updates). Nothing measures the diversity cost of mid-round sharing on ideation; blackboard papers report gains but only against whole-system baselines.

### Cited Findings
- "Passes Alone, Fails Together" (STALE benchmark, preprint, EXPRESS '26): on 36 constructed Django instances with GPT-5.5 (216 runs), blind merges of individually passing patches showed interference in 105 of 108 runs (97%); a template-generated message of about 130 tokens describing the other agent's completed change recovered 89 of 108 runs (82%), and 93% among cleanly merging cases; on the synthetic tier mean interference fell 2.50 to 0.04 (98%). Mined real PR pairs rarely reproduce it (1 of 834 runs), which the authors attribute to reviewed history containing few unresolved parallel interactions. Shared context, locks and worktrees were not evaluated; messages were oracle, assuming "complete, accurate descriptions", [arXiv 2609.25396](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.25396)
- "When Sub-Agents Work in Parallel" (preprint, Codex on GPT-5.4, Claude Code on Opus 5, Kimi Code on K3; 354 long-horizon tasks plus 100 SWE-bench Verified, 2,124 executions, $20,780): resolution effects ranged from an 11.9 point decrease to a 2.3 point increase; Claude Code's SWE-bench Verified pass rate fell 83.0% to 59.0% with concurrency on (p = 5.1e-7), Kimi 77.0% to 59.0% (p = 0.013), Codex unchanged at 71.0%; LoopsBench rose up to 14.3 points for Codex and Claude Code. Tokens 1.41 to 3.31x sequential; runtime rose in 14 of 15 agent-benchmark pairs; concurrency was faster in 27.9% of tasks solved both ways. Failure taxonomy (804 instances, kappa 0.77): shared state and merge 33.2%, of which concurrent writes 26.5% (deliverable overwrite 14.9%, same-file collision 5.2%) and shared environment contamination 6.7%; execution governance 29.0% (early sub-agent termination 15.9%); task orchestration 28.5% (fan-out budget exhaustion 19.2%); unchanged retry 3.1%. There is no "duplicated work" category, [arXiv 2610.10263](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.10263)
- Anthropic (vendor): duplication from vague delegation (three subagents, two on the same 2025 supply chain topic) and "agents distracting each other with excessive updates" as named failure modes; subagents cannot coordinate with each other under synchronous execution, [Anthropic engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)
- Claude Code agent teams docs: "Two teammates editing the same file leads to overwrites. Break the work so each teammate owns a different set of files"; "For sequential tasks, same-file edits, or work with many dependencies, a single session or subagents are more effective"; "Three focused teammates often outperform five scattered ones", [Claude Code agent teams](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-teams)
- AgentRoom (preprint): file-level claims plus a broadcast log so agents negotiate ownership before editing; reports zero semantic conflicts in its headline pool against a 5 to 10% semantic conflict rate for CodeCRDT, [arXiv 2608.23740](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.23740). Claim Plane: a 30-pair, three-seed, 360-execution confirmatory study of deterministic pre-write admission; title says "reliability gains and the limits of selective concurrency", [arXiv 2608.00947](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.00947) (title and abstract only)
- A blog reports a study of 33,596 agent-authored PRs across 2,807 repositories finding a 41.7% cross-agent conflict rate, about twice the intra-agent rate. Secondary, primary paper not located, [codex.danielvaughan.com](https://codex.danielvaughan.com/2026/07/28/agent-pr-merge-conflicts-concurrent-coding-agents-codex-cli-worktree-isolation-coordination-defence/)
- Blackboard systems: LbMAS removes each agent's private memory in favour of the board, has a control unit choose the next specialist and a "cleaner" that prunes redundant messages; an aggregator reports 4.33 to 5.02% over CoT and static baselines on MMLU and GPQA and 72.60% on MATH at 4.72M tokens, [arXiv 2507.01701](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.01701), numbers via [Emergent Mind](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/blackboard-based-llm-multi-agent-system-bmas) (unverified against the tables). A blackboard for data discovery reports 13 to 57% relative task success over master-slave baselines, and defines the split: blackboard broadcasts requests and each agent decides whether to take part, shared memory has a coordinator assign, [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=egTQgf89Lm)
- PatchBoard (preprint): plain and structured blackboards solve fewer ALFWorld episodes than schema-grounded state mutation with write contracts and validation, and the plain blackboard has a much higher cost per success, [arXiv 2605.29313](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.29313)
- Cross-paper comparison of memory designs is unreliable because each benchmark uses its own data, metrics and protocol, [arXiv 2603.07670](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.07670)
- Co-scientist's between-round condensation: meta-review feedback is appended to prompts in the next iteration and the Generation agent uses it "selectively to avoid overfitting", [arXiv 2502.18864v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18864v1)

### Inferences
- The two duplication and conflict incidents in the run are the two failure classes these papers measure: a stale interface (two patches on one function) and overlapping scope (two model swaps). STALE's result says a 130-token "I changed X" message, not a shared board, closes the first class almost entirely, and only once the change is complete. The second class is a lead-side problem in Anthropic's account (brief precision), not a channel problem.
- Mid-round reading has a measured cost (2610.10263's SWE-bench declines, Anthropic's distraction) and a measured benefit only for declared, completed changes. Interim hypotheses are neither. A board that accepts only "claimed scope" and "done, interface changed" entries, read by the lead and by workers at a checkpoint, is what the evidence supports; a free blackboard is what the blackboard papers show working only with a cleaner and a controller.
- No source measures diversity loss from sharing on ideation. MoA's single-proposer ablation (61.3 vs 56.7) and MultiAgentBench's "group discussion scores worst" are the nearest signals that independent generation beats shared generation.

### Gaps
- No measurement of a shared board's effect on hypothesis diversity or on premature convergence in an optimisation loop.
- No comparison of "share interim findings" against "share condensed findings between rounds" on the same task.
- The blackboard accuracy numbers come from an aggregator and I did not confirm them against LbMAS's tables.

## How production frameworks implement coordination as of 2026, and what their docs warn about

### Takeaway
Every mainstream framework has converged on isolated contexts plus a narrow channel: a returned result (subagents, agents-as-tools), a transferred conversation (handoffs), a shared reducer-merged state (LangGraph), a broadcast thread with a model-chosen speaker (AutoGen), or a file-locked task list plus mailboxes (Claude Code agent teams). The warnings are about overwrites, context growth, unbounded loops and tokens, and none of the docs claims a measured gain.

### Cited Findings
- Claude Code agent teams (experimental, off by default): lead plus teammates, each "in its own context window", teammates "message each other directly"; a shared task list with pending, in progress, completed states and dependencies that auto-unblock; "Task claiming uses file locking to prevent race conditions"; mailboxes are JSON files at `~/.claude/teams/{team}/inboxes/{agent}.json`; the lead's conversation history does not carry over; hooks `TeammateIdle`, `TaskCreated`, `TaskCompleted` can refuse with exit code 2. Warnings: "significantly more tokens than a single session", "Two teammates editing the same file leads to overwrites", "Task status can lag", no nested teams, lead is fixed, start with 3 to 5 teammates and 5 to 6 tasks each, "Letting a team run unattended for too long increases the risk of wasted effort", and the lead "can stop early too". Messages from another agent are flagged as not from the user and cannot carry approvals, [Claude Code agent teams](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-teams)
- Claude Code's comparison table: subagents "Return a result to the caller", agent teams "Self-coordination through messages, plus a shared task list"; "Use subagents when you need quick, focused workers that report back. Use agent teams when teammates need to share findings, challenge each other, and coordinate on their own", [Claude Code agent teams](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-teams)
- OpenAI Agents SDK handoffs: a handoff is a tool (`transfer_to_x`) that moves control; "By default, the receiving agent sees the entire previous conversation history"; `input_filter` controls what the next agent sees and `remove_all_tools` strips tool items; nested handoff history is an opt-in beta that condenses earlier history into summaries. Warnings: guardrails apply only to the first and final agents, nested history "does not redact sensitive data". Agents-as-tools (`Agent.as_tool`) is recommended when the primary agent should keep the conversation and call a specialist, [OpenAI Agents SDK](https://openai.github.io/openai-agents-python/handoffs/)
- LangGraph: parallel nodes writing the same state key in one step raise `INVALID_CONCURRENT_GRAPH_UPDATE`; the fix is a reducer on the key (`Annotated[list, add_messages]` or `operator.add`), and subgraphs give workers isolated state, [LangChain docs](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langgraph/errors/INVALID_CONCURRENT_GRAPH_UPDATE)
- AutoGen SelectorGroupChat: each speaker's response is "broadcasted to all other participants", a model picks the next speaker from the shared history and participant descriptions, and history persists across tasks until `reset()`. Warnings: "Try not to overload the model with too much instruction in the selector prompt", termination is the user's job (the example pairs a text trigger with `MaxMessageTermination(max_messages=25)` "to avoid infinite loop"), and the page gives no guidance on context growth though the full history feeds every selector call, [AutoGen docs](https://microsoft.github.io/autogen/stable/user-guide/agentchat-user-guide/selector-group-chat.html)
- Worktree isolation is the common practitioner answer for parallel coding agents; it prevents text-level collisions, not semantic ones, [Augment guide](https://www.augmentcode.com/guides/how-to-run-a-multi-agent-coding-workspace), [Mindstudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/git-worktrees-parallel-ai-coding-agents), [arXiv 2605.15221](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.15221)
- Protocol-level comparison exists: ProtocolBench scores agent protocols on task success, latency, message overhead and robustness under failure, [arXiv 2510.17149](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.17149) (search snippet only)

### Inferences
- The run's board (`board.py` with `say`, `hold`, `release`) is a hand-built version of what Claude Code's agent teams ship: a file-locked claim plus a mailbox. The difference is that agent teams let the lead refuse a task completion through a hook, which is a cheap place to put a "did you declare your interface change" check.
- Every framework's warning is about the same three things: same-file writes, unbounded shared history, and the lead quitting or racing ahead. None of them warns about diversity, which matches the literature gap above.

### Gaps
- CrewAI docs were not fetched; I have no primary source on its coordination primitives.
- No framework publishes a measured comparison of its own coordination modes.

## Debate, agreement and truth: sycophancy and convergence to the majority

### Takeaway
On homogeneous small-model teams, unguided debate loses to isolated self-correction in every tested configuration while costing 2 to 3x the tokens, correct agents abandon correct answers at rates up to 70%, and models that state the majority answer can still internally hold their original one. Agreement between agents of the same family is a weak signal of truth; the measured fixes are telling agents who is sycophantic, auditing reasoning trees instead of counting votes, and capping rounds.

### Cited Findings
- "The Cost of Consensus" (preprint; Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, Ministral-3-8B; N=10 agents, R=3 rounds; GSM-Hard and MMLU-Hard): round-3 accuracy, debate vs isolated self-correction, GSM-Hard Qwen 58.8 vs 61.0, Ministral 20.7 vs 48.3, Llama 39.3 vs 41.0; MMLU-Hard Qwen 60.7 vs 66.7, Ministral 62.0 vs 65.3, Llama 45.0 vs 47.7. Self-correction matched or beat debate in all six, significant for Qwen and Ministral. Debate used 2.1 to 3.4x the tokens (Qwen GSM-Hard 18,240 vs 5,396). Modal sycophancy reached 85.5% (Qwen, MMLU-Hard); vulnerability rate (initially correct agents abandoning a correct answer after peer exposure) reached 70.0% (Ministral, GSM-Hard); the oracle gap, a correct answer in the pool that plurality voting discarded, reached 32.3 points. Scope: unguided, homogeneous 7 to 8B teams; no roles, no heterogeneity, no reasoning models, [arXiv 2605.00914](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.00914)
- "Too Polite to Disagree" (preprint; six Llama and Qwen models 3B to 32B, n=6 agents, 5 rounds, MMLU with the user endorsing a wrong option): majority accuracy 0.804 baseline vs 0.909 when agents see a sycophancy ranking of their peers, "an absolute 10.5 percentage point increase"; majority sycophancy fell 0.196 to 0.108; without rankings "the accuracy trajectories drop" over rounds; random and accuracy-based priors reached only 0.863 to 0.891; in the baseline the smaller Llama models exert more influence than the more reliable Qwen models, [arXiv 2604.02668](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02668)
- "Silent Dissent" (preprint, pre-registered; Qwen3.5-4B, Qwen3.6-27B, Gemma-4-E4B, Llama-3.1-8B; two-hop questions with scripted unanimous wrong peers): among agents that gave in, the correct bridge entity was still readable from the residual stream (hit@100 above control 0.85 for Qwen3.5-4B, 0.22 and 0.24 for the others); Qwen3.5-4B gave in on 89% of questions when its earlier answer was hidden versus 8% when visible. "Stated consensus in multi-agent debate can thus overstate agreement", [arXiv 2610.02702](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02702)
- Theory and related: Estornell and Liu (2024) formalise convergence of similarly capable agents to an incorrect majority; Wynn et al. report debate "can decrease accuracy over time, even when stronger models outnumber weaker ones"; Yao et al. (2025) attribute premature consensus to "alignment training that prioritises agreeableness" , all as cited in [arXiv 2604.07667](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.07667) and [arXiv 2605.30391](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.30391). Debate does help in some settings (Du et al. 2024, Liang et al. 2024) and "its gains depend on configuration" (Smit et al. 2024) , same sources
- "Not All Flips Are Conformity" decomposes stance convergence in debate into conformity and genuine updating, [arXiv 2606.00820](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.00820) (title and snippet only)
- Deception in mixture of LLMs: single-layer ablations "lead to insignificant changes in performance", and the setup is vulnerable to a deceptive proposer, [arXiv 2503.05856](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.05856)
- Practitioner guidance: cap debate rounds because "uncapped debate degrades accuracy as sycophantic convergence compounds", and assign a separate agent to find flaws in the consensus, [tianpan.co](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026/04/12/consensus-protocols-multi-agent-decisions-when-agents-disagree)

### Inferences
- The run's design, where ideation agents never see each other and skeptics are read-only, is the configuration the debate literature says to keep. The thing it must not add is a round where workers or skeptics see each other's verdicts before committing their own, because that is where the 70% vulnerability and 85% modal sycophancy numbers come from.
- "Two workers recommended keeping it" is not two votes. Both ran the same model family on the same brief, so their agreement is the correlated-agent case the theory papers describe. Reproducibility of the skeptic's refusal is the independent signal; agreement is not.
- If agreement is ever used as a gate, the measured fixes are: weight by a known sycophancy score, favour a reproducible minority over a stated majority (AgentAuditor), and cap rounds.

### Gaps
- All the strongest sycophancy results are on 3B to 32B open models; nothing comparable measured on frontier models in a multi-agent engineering loop.
- No measurement of whether heterogeneous model families in debate avoid the correlated-convergence problem, only the theory that similarity causes it.

## Known failure modes, mapped to the topologies

### Takeaway
Each structure has a measured or vendor-observed failure signature; the list below is what the sources support, with duplication and distraction attributed to hub and spoke with vague briefs, shared-state corruption to concurrent writes, premature convergence and sycophancy to debate, and self-rating drift to tournaments.

### Cited Findings
- Duplicated work: vague delegation in hub and spoke (Anthropic), [Anthropic engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system); step repetition is MAST's single largest mode at 15.7%, [arXiv 2503.13657v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v3)
- Agents distracting each other: "excessive updates" (Anthropic), [Anthropic engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system); "information overload" from a global pool (AgileCoder), [arXiv 2406.11912](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.11912); group discussion worst on all MultiAgentBench metrics, [arXiv 2503.01935v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01935v1)
- Shared-state corruption: concurrent writes 26.5% of failure instances, shared environment contamination 6.7%, [arXiv 2610.10263](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.10263); stale-interface interference in 97% of constructed blind merges, [arXiv 2609.25396](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.25396); LangGraph refuses unreduced concurrent writes outright, [LangChain docs](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langgraph/errors/INVALID_CONCURRENT_GRAPH_UPDATE)
- Premature convergence and sycophantic agreement: vulnerability rate up to 70.0%, modal sycophancy 85.5%, oracle gap 32.3 points, [arXiv 2605.00914](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.00914); 89% conformity with answer hidden, [arXiv 2610.02702](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02702)
- Self-rating drift in tournaments: "the Elo metric is auto-evaluated and not based on independent ground truth", [arXiv 2502.18864v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18864v1); Pass@250 above 30% but Pass@2 of 19.2% shows search finding answers its selector does not pick, [Sakana AB-MCTS](https://sakana.ai/ab-mcts/)
- Over-rejection by critics: FNR up to 87.9% for GPT-4o under full prompting on MBPP, [arXiv 2603.00539](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00539)
- Lead failures: lead stops early or starts implementing instead of waiting; task status lags and blocks dependents, [Claude Code agent teams](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-teams); premature termination 6.20% and unaware of termination conditions 12.4%, [arXiv 2503.13657v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v3)
- Verification gaps: incorrect verification 9.10% plus no or incomplete verification 8.20%, [arXiv 2503.13657v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v3); early sub-agent termination 15.9% of concurrency failures, [arXiv 2610.10263](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.10263)

### Inferences
- The two failures the run actually hit (duplicated model swap, conflicting patches on one function) are the two best-measured ones, and both have a measured, cheap fix: a precise brief with an exclusion list from the lead, and a completed-change message on a claim board that the merge step reads. Neither needs mid-round peer messaging.
- The failure the run did not hit, and should keep not hitting, is sycophantic convergence. Keeping ideation blind and skeptics read-only is the measured protection.

### Gaps
- No single study runs the same engineering task across all seven shapes; every comparison above is between two or three.
- No source quantifies the diversity cost of a shared board on an ideation stage.
